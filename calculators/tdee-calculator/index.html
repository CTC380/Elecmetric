How this was calculated

    How the activity factor is built

    The activity factor is your working-day base plus an exercise add-on, capped at 1.9.

    Working dayBaseExerciseAdd-on
    Mostly sitting1.200None+0.000
    Some walking or standing1.3001 to 3 light sessions a week+0.175
    On your feet most of the day1.4003 to 5 moderate sessions a week+0.350
    Heavy physical work1.5006 to 7 hard sessions a week+0.525
    Very hard daily or twice-daily training+0.700

    For someone with a desk job, this reproduces the widely used factors 1.2, 1.375, 1.55, 1.725 and 1.9. The job bases (1.3, 1.4, 1.5) are Elecmetric planning assumptions, not measured values. They are there to stop a physical job being treated like a desk job. TDEE = resting energy × activity factor.

    Limitations

    • TDEE from an equation is an estimate, not a measurement. Individual errors of 10% or more are common.
    • The activity factor is an assumption. Choosing the wrong activity level is the largest source of error, usually an overestimate.
    • The realistic range uses one activity band (0.175) either side of your factor, limited to 1.2 and 1.9. It shows how sensitive the result is to your activity choice. It is not a statistical confidence interval.
    • Daily energy use changes with body weight, adaptation to diet, training and illness.
    • This is not medical advice. Speak to a doctor or dietitian before large dietary changes, especially with a medical condition or a history of disordered eating.
