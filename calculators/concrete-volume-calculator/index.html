Estimate net concrete volume for slabs, footings, columns, walls, beams, circular and hollow sections, stairs, curbs, ramps, trenches and piles. Formulas used

Every dimension is converted to metres first, the volume is computed in m³ at full precision, and only the displayed numbers are rounded. 1 m³ = 1,000 litres = 35.3147 ft³ = 1.30795 yd³; 1 yd³ = 27 ft³; cft is the same unit as ft³.

    Assumptions and limits

    • Stairs. “Solid stepped” fills every step to the ground: V = W × run × rise × n(n+1)/2, plus an optional solid top platform. “Waist-slab” is a sloping slab of thickness t plus the triangular step fillets. These are different structures and are not interchangeable. Add a separate stair landing element where one is cast separately.
    • Curb and gutter. Cross-section = curb depth × curb height + gutter width × flag thickness. Curb height is the full height from underside of the flag; gutter width is measured out from the curb face, so nothing is counted twice. Check your drawing uses the same definitions.
    • Piles. The bell is a conical frustum from the bell diameter up to the shaft diameter. Real excavations are rarely this regular, and concrete over-break is not included.
    • Beams and slabs. Beams are measured to overall depth. If a beam is cast monolithically with a slab, deduct the overlapping slab depth yourself so concrete is not counted twice.
    • Density. There is no universal value. The default of 2,400 kg/m³ is a commonly used assumption, not a measured one. Use the figure in your specification. Weight is hidden when the density field is empty or invalid.
    • Reinforcement displacement, formwork leakage, spillage and over-excavation are not deducted or added here.

    FAQ

    How do I calculate concrete for a slab?

    Multiply length × width × thickness in the same unit. 5 m × 4 m × 0.15 m = 3.00 m³. Add waste in Mode 02.

    How many cubic feet is one cubic metre?

    1 m³ = 35.3147 ft³. In India ft³ is usually written cft.

    Why is my volume different from another calculator?

    Usually units, the way stairs or curbs are defined, or whether openings and overlaps were deducted. Open “Calculation steps” to see exactly what was used.
