    How the solver works

    To find the exponent in 2x = 10, take logarithms: x ln 2 = ln 10, so x = ln 10 / ln 2. A whole number or a fraction is accepted only if it is proved with whole numbers (a^p = y^q); otherwise the result is an approximation, as here.

    The problem

    a = 2
y = 10
Solve a^n = y for n

    Take the logarithm of both sides

    a^n = y
n · ln(a) = ln(y)
n = ln(y) / ln(a)
(Any logarithm base gives the same ratio; natural logarithms are used here.)

    Calculate

    ln(2) = 0.6931471806
ln(10) = 2.302585093
n = 2.302585093 / 0.6931471806 = 3.32192809489

    Exact value

    n is not a whole number or a fraction with a denominator up to 1000 that satisfies a^n = y exactly, so it is irrational (or very close to a fraction with a larger denominator): the decimal is a double-precision approximation (about 15 reliable digits).

    Result

    n = ≈ 3.32193

    To find the base in x2 = 12, take the square root and try both signs through the real-power rule. The root is simplified with the same method as the Negative & Fractional Exponents mode.

    The problem

    n = 2
y = 12
Solve a^n = y for a

    Take the root

    a^n = y
a = y^(1/n)
n = 2 (reduced), so 1/n = 1/2
|a| = |y|^(1/2)

    How many real solutions

    The reduced exponent has an even numerator (2) and an odd denominator, so a and −a give the same positive value: a^n is never negative, and when y > 0 there are two real bases.

    Evaluate the root

    |a| = |y|^(1/2) is worked out with the fractional-exponent method (the steps below call that exponent n).

    Fractional exponent: a root and a power

    a^(p/q) = (square root of a)^p = square root of (a^p)
Here p = 1 and q = 2: the denominator q is the root, the numerator p is the power.

    The root, then the power

    The square root of 12 is ≈ 3.4641   (not a ratio of whole numbers)
Raise it to the power 1

    Factor the base into primes

    The magnitude 12 = 2² × 3

    Multiply each prime’s exponent by n

    2²  →  exponent 2 × 1/2 = 1  =  1
3  →  exponent 1 × 1/2 = 1/2  =  0 + 1/2
The whole part comes out of the root. The fractional part stays inside it.

    Outside and inside the root

    Outside: 2 = 2
Inside the square root: 3 = 3

    Result

    a = 2√3  or  a = −2√3   (≈ ±3.4641)

    Rules and limits

    • Base, exponent and result may be whole numbers, decimals, fractions such as 3/4, numbers with a power of ten such as 1e-3, or negative. The base may also be the constant e when you solve for the exponent or the result.
    • An exponent found by the solver is shown as an exact fraction when its denominator is up to 1,000 and its numerator up to 4,000 and a^p = y^q holds exactly; otherwise it is a double-precision approximation.
    • A negative base is judged by the reduced exponent: whole number or odd denominator gives a real power with the sign of (−1)^p; an even denominator has no real value.
    • 0^0, 0 to a negative power and 0^n = y for y ≠ 0 are reported as indeterminate or having no solution, never silently given as 1 or infinity.
    • Complex solutions (for example x^2 = −4, x = ±2i) are not part of this mode.

    Questions

    What does the exponent solver do?

    It solves a^n = y when you know two of the three values. Know the base and the exponent and it gives the result; know the base and the result and it gives the exponent; know the exponent and the result and it gives the base. It also tells you whether the answer is unique, has several real values, does not exist or is indeterminate.

    How do I find the exponent in 2^x = 32, and what if it is not a whole number or a fraction?

    Take logarithms of both sides: x ln 2 = ln 32, so x = ln 32 / ln 2 = 5. The solver finds whole-number and fractional answers exactly and proves them with whole-number arithmetic, so 4^x = 8 gives n = 3/2 and not 1.5000000001. What if the exponent is not a whole number or a fraction? Then it is an irrational number such as log2(10), and the solver gives a decimal approximation and says so: n ≈ 3.32193 (to 6 digits). About 15 digits are reliable.

    How do I find the base in x^3 = 125?

    Take the n-th root: x = 125^(1/3) = 5. The solver writes the answer as an exact whole number, fraction or simplified radical (x^2 = 12 gives a = 2√3 or a = −2√3) and also shows the decimal.

    Why does x^2 = 9 give two answers?

    An even power hides the sign: 3^2 = 9 and (−3)^2 = 9. The solver lists both: a = 3 or a = −3. For an odd power such as x^3 = −27 there is one answer, a = −3.

    Why is there no solution to 2^x = −8?

    A positive base raised to any real power is positive, so 2^x can never be −8. The solver reports “no real solution” instead of returning an error or a misleading number. Complex exponents are not part of this mode.

    Can the base be negative, and what does “indeterminate” mean?

    Yes, when the power is a real number: a whole-number exponent, or a reduced fraction with an odd denominator. (−2)^x = −8 gives n = 3 and (−8)^x = −2 gives n = 1/3, but (−2)^x = 8 has no real solution, because the sign would be wrong. What does “indeterminate” mean? Every value works, or the expression is undefined. 1^x = 1 is true for every x, a^0 = 1 is true for every non-zero a, and 0^0 is undefined, so the missing value cannot be found from the equation.

    How is the exponent found when the base is e?

    x = ln y, because ln e = 1: e^x = 10 gives n ≈ 2.30259. Type the letter e as the base.

    Can I type fractions and decimals, and how accurate is the answer?

    Yes. 1/2, 0.5 and 5e-1 are the same number. An exponent of 2/3 is read exactly, never as 0.6666667, so x^(2/3) = 4 gives a = 8 or a = −8. How accurate is the answer? Whole numbers, fractions and radicals are exact. Irrational exponents and decimals are double-precision approximations with about 15 reliable digits, labelled as approximate. Every answer is put back into a^n and checked.

    Where is the power calculator?

    Mode 01 (Power Calculator) computes a^n exactly for whole-number exponents, and Mode 02 (Negative & Fractional Exponents) gives radical forms. This mode works backwards from the result.

    The calculation runs in your browser, and the history list is stored only in your browser. This page also loads Google Tag Manager; data collected by tags depends on its configuration.
