1 kV = 1,000 V

Common conversions

  • 1 kV = 1,000 V
  • 1 V = 0.001 kV
  • 1 A = 1,000 mA
  • 1 kΩ = 1,000 Ω
  • 1 kW = 1,000 W
  • 1 kWh = 3,600,000 J

Units change, quantities do not

A unit conversion multiplies by a fixed factor and stays inside one quantity. Volts, amps and ohms are three different quantities, so there is no factor between them. Ohm's law links them and needs two of the three.

Power has three kinds

Watts (kW) are real power, volt-amperes (kVA) are apparent power and VAR (kVAR) are reactive power. kVA to kW needs the power factor, so it is not a unit conversion.

Amps to kW needs voltage, power factor and phases

Current alone does not give power. Use the amps to kW calculator, which covers DC, single-phase and three-phase, line-to-line or line-to-neutral voltage, power factor and efficiency, and shows the working.

Unit factors

Every unit in terms of the base unit shown. To convert, multiply by the factor of the unit you have and divide by the factor of the unit you want.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula to convert kV to V?

V = kV × 1,000. For example, 5 kV is 5,000 V.

How do I convert V back to kV?

kV = V × 0.001. The Swap button above reverses the direction for any pair of units.

Can this page convert volts to amps?

No. Volts and amps are different quantities. Current depends on the load: use Ohm's law with a resistance, or the kW to amps calculator with a power, voltage, power factor and number of phases.
