To add a calculator to the independent set, add its textbook formula to `gen_vectors.py` **without reading engine.js**, regenerate, and commit the JSON. A mismatch means one side is wrong; resolve it against a published worked example.

Coverage: every one of the 246 calculators now has independent vectors. The calculator added in v1.99.10 (railway-backup-load, 42 vectors) is written as a different route from the engine's closed forms, in exact fractions and 30-digit mpmath: each load group is taken on its own as the current it draws from the battery bank (rated watts × duty × growth ÷ efficiency ÷ bus voltage) and multiplied by the backup hours to give ampere-hours, where the engine adds the watts for the whole bank and divides by the voltage at the end; the nameplate capacity is the ampere-hours over the product of the three capacity factors, the standard size is the first entry of the sorted list at or above it, the runtime of an installed bank is found by bisection on the hours until the ampere-hours drawn equal the usable ampere-hours, and the charger current is the recharge current plus the bus current. Conversion Centre note: since v1.8.2 the engine uses the exact definitions (mechanical horsepower 745.69987158 W, International Table BTU 1055.05585262 J) and the independent factors in the test use the same ones, so the two sides agree to rounding. The 1e-8 comparison tolerance is kept for the other units.

The calculator added in v1.94.3 (pt-ratio, 29 vectors) is written from the definitions without reading the engine: K = Vpn / Vsn, the actual ratio is the test primary over the test secondary, the ratio error is (K Vts - Vtp) / Vtp in per cent, and the burden loading is Sc / Sn; the test and burden outputs are left out when their inputs are 0.

The calculator added in v1.94.4 (diode-circuit, 70 vectors) is written from the textbook relations without reading the engine, by a different route: the DC circuit with the Shockley diode is solved in closed form with the Lambert W function (not by iteration), and the rectifier waveform vo = Vpk sin t - Vt is integrated numerically for its average and mean square (not from the closed form). Vt is Vf for one diode and 2 Vf for a bridge; the bridge PIV is Vpk - Vf, the half-wave PIV is Vpk; the clamp output runs from -Vf to 2 Vpk - Vf. Outputs that do not apply to a circuit are left out.
