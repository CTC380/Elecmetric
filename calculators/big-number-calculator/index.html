How to use the Big Number Calculator

  1. Type Number A. Digits only, with an optional sign. You can paste 1,234,567 with commas, or write 23E18 for 23 followed by 18 zeros.
  2. Choose the operation: + − × ÷ MOD, Power or Square.
  3. Type Number B (or the exponent). Square needs only A.
  4. Read the result. Division gives a quotient and a remainder. Every result shows its digit count, scientific and engineering notation, digit sum, and first and last digits. Use “Copy digits” to copy the exact value without grouping commas.

The calculator also writes out the working where that is practical: column addition and subtraction, partial products for multiplication, long division digit by digit, and square-and-multiply for powers.

Why an ordinary calculator is not enough

A normal calculator, a spreadsheet and plain JavaScript hold numbers as 64-bit floating-point values. They are exact for whole numbers only up to 9,007,199,254,740,991 (253 − 1). Above that, some integers cannot be stored at all and the last digits are replaced by zeros or by the nearest value that can.

For example, 264 in floating point displays as 18446744073709552000, but the exact value is 18446744073709551616. And 9007199254740993 becomes 9007199254740992. This calculator uses arbitrary-length integers (BigInt), so the digits are all real.

Division with a quotient and a remainder

Dividing whole numbers does not always give a whole number. Instead of hiding the fractional part in a rounded decimal, this calculator gives the quotient and the remainder, so that A = B × quotient + remainder. For 100 ÷ 7 that is 14 remainder 2, because 7 × 14 + 2 = 100. It also shows the exact value as a reduced fraction. A decimal expansion to a chosen number of places is planned for the Big Decimal mode (Mode 02).

MOD and negative numbers

A MOD B is the remainder when A is divided by B. For positive numbers every method agrees: 17 MOD 5 = 2. For a negative number there are three common conventions, and software does not agree on them. The calculator lets you choose, and it states the one in use.

  • Euclidean (default): the remainder is always 0 or more and less than |B|. −17 MOD 5 = 3.
  • Floored: the quotient is rounded down, so the remainder takes the sign of B (the rule of Python’s % operator).
  • Truncated: the quotient is cut toward zero, so the remainder takes the sign of A (the rule of JavaScript’s % and of C).

Pick the convention your software or textbook uses before comparing results. A MOD 0 is undefined.

Powers and squares

Powers use repeated squaring, which needs about log2 of the exponent steps instead of one multiplication per unit of the exponent. Because powers grow very fast, results are limited to 20,000 digits and the size is checked before anything is computed, so the page cannot freeze. 101000 is fine; 2100000 (about 30,103 digits) is refused with a message. The exponent must be a whole number of 0 or more. 00 is taken as 1. A negative exponent would give a fraction, so it is outside this whole-number mode.

Common mistakes

  • Trusting a floating-point result above 9,007,199,254,740,991. The last digits may be wrong.
  • Reading 1.2e3 as a decimal. Here it means 1200. A value such as 1.5e0 has a fractional part and is refused in this mode.
  • Assuming MOD of a negative number matches another program. Check the convention.
  • Dropping the remainder. 100 ÷ 7 is not 14. It is 14 with a remainder of 2.

Limits and how results are checked

  • Input: up to 5,000 digits per number (after expanding E-notation), 0 to 9 and one sign. Decimals and fractions are refused with a message.
  • Result: up to 20,000 digits. A larger power is refused before it is computed. The largest exponent is 1,000,000.
  • Exact, never rounded: every answer is an exact integer. Scientific notation shown beside it is rounded to 10 significant digits (half up) and says so; the full digits are always shown.
  • Checks on every result: the inverse operation, casting out nines and elevens, a modular reduction that does not build the big number, and for small exponents repeated multiplication.
  • Privacy: the calculation runs in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server. The history list is stored only in this browser and you can clear it.

Arbitrary precision is not unlimited computing. Very long inputs and results take more time and memory, which is why the limits above exist.

Where large exact integers matter

Exact whole-number arithmetic is used wherever a rounded answer is not acceptable: checking the digits of a long identifier or serial number, counting combinations, testing a calculation done in a spreadsheet or a script, working with the large integers of checksums and hashes, and teaching how rounding error enters a calculation. A large number alone does not identify an engineering quantity: keep the unit with the number in your own records, and use the dedicated calculators on this site for electrical quantities.

Questions

How do I calculate with very large numbers?

Type the first number, choose the operation, then type the second number. The calculator handles whole numbers of up to 5,000 digits and shows every digit of the exact answer.

Is the answer exact or rounded?

Exact. Whole-number results are never rounded. Only the scientific notation shown beside the result is rounded to 10 significant digits, and the page labels it.

Why does my phone calculator give a different result?

Most calculators hold numbers as floating-point values, which keep about 15 to 17 significant digits. Beyond that the last digits are lost. This calculator keeps every digit.

What does 23E18 mean?

It is E-notation for 23 × 10^18, which is 23 followed by 18 zeros: 23,000,000,000,000,000,000. It is accepted when the value is a whole number.

How are remainders handled for negative numbers?

You choose. Euclidean (the default) never gives a negative remainder, floored follows the sign of B, and truncated follows the sign of A. The page lists all three for the numbers you enter.

What is the largest power I can calculate?

Any power whose result has at most 20,000 digits, with an exponent up to 1,000,000. For example 10^1000 and 2^50000 work; 2^100000 does not.

Can it do factorials, GCD, LCM or square roots?

Not in this mode. Big Integer Arithmetic is Mode 01. Power, root and factorial, number theory and numerical analysis are later modes. For GCF and LCM of large numbers, use the GCF Calculator.

Can it divide to a number of decimal places?

Not yet. This mode gives the quotient, the remainder and the exact fraction. Decimal division with a chosen precision is planned for the Big Decimal mode (Mode 02).

Is anything sent to a server?

No. The calculation runs in your browser. The history list is stored only in this browser and you can clear it.
