What a factor is and how to find all of them

A factor (or divisor) of a whole number n is a whole number that divides n exactly, leaving no remainder. 6 is a factor of 36 because 36 ÷ 6 = 6 exactly; 5 is not, because 36 ÷ 5 = 7 remainder 1. Every number has 1 and itself as factors.

Method 1: test every number up to the square root

Divide n by 1, 2, 3, and so on. Each time the division is exact, you have found two factors at once: the divisor and the quotient. You can stop at the square root of n, because any factor larger than that has a partner smaller than it. For 36 the square root is 6, so test 1 to 6:

Pairing each found factor with its quotient gives the full list: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36.

Method 2: build them from the prime factorisation (what this calculator does)

Write the number as a product of primes, then take every possible combination of the prime powers. For 360 = 23 × 32 × 5, choose a power of 2 from 20 to 23, a power of 3 from 30 to 32 and a power of 5 from 50 to 51:

Factor pairs

A factor pair is two factors whose product is the number. The smallest factor pairs with the largest, the next smallest with the next largest, and so on. For 36:

13636
21836

That is 5 positive factor pairs. A number has an equal pair (here 6 × 6) exactly when it is a perfect square, which is also why perfect squares are the numbers with an odd number of factors. Pairs are useful for arranging items in equal rows and columns.

Negative numbers and zero

Negative numbers. The positive factors of −36 are the same as those of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36. What changes is the signed pairs, because a product of −36 needs exactly one negative number. Zero. Every nonzero integer divides 0, because 0 = k × 0 for any k. The list of factors of 0 would be 1, 2, 3, … without end, so 0 has no finite factor list and the calculator explains this instead of listing anything. Zero is also never a factor of another number, because division by 0 is undefined.

Prime, composite, and the special case of 1

A number above 1 is prime if it has exactly two positive factors, 1 and itself, and composite if it has more than two. The number 1 has only one factor, so it is neither.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up factors and multiples. Factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 (they divide 12). Multiples of 12 are 12, 24, 36, … (12 divides them).
  • Mixing up all factors and prime factors. 12 has six factors but only two prime factors, 2 and 3. Its prime factorisation is 22 × 3.
  • Stopping the search too early or too late. You need only test up to the square root, but you must then add each partner.
  • Forgetting 1 and the number itself. Both are always factors.
  • Thinking 1 is prime. It is not: it has one factor, not two.

Rules this calculator follows

  • One nonzero integer. Decimals, fractions and lists of numbers are refused with a message saying where to go instead. 36.0 is read as 36.
  • Exact integers. The number is held in BigInt arithmetic, so there is no floating-point rounding anywhere in the result.
  • Limit: 1018. Every number up to 19 digits is factored in full, in well under a second. The most factors any of them has is 103,680. Larger numbers are refused rather than guessed, because factoring a big number can take very long and its factor list can be larger than a page can show.
  • No factor is missed. The list is generated from the prime factorisation, not by trying numbers, and primes are confirmed with a Miller–Rabin test that is exact in this range.
  • Checked every time. Every factor divides the number, the count equals (a + 1)(b + 1)…, the pairs multiply back, the factors add up to the divisor-sum formula, and, up to 1012, a separate trial division of every number to the square root finds the same count.
  • Big lists. A number with more than 2,000 factors shows the first and last 100 on the page; Copy factors and Save as .csv always give the complete list.

The right grouping of cells, panels or fittings is set by the voltage, current, string and mismatch limits of the real equipment, which come from its data sheet and the applicable design rules, not from the factor list. Factors also show common sizes: the gear teeth or pulley diameters that share a factor mesh in a short repeating pattern, and a count with many factors can be split into equal groups in many ways.

Questions

How do I find all the factors of a number?

Divide the number by 1, 2, 3 and so on up to its square root. Every exact division gives two factors: the divisor and the quotient. Or write the number as a product of primes and multiply every combination of the prime powers. The factors of 36 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18 and 36.

How many factors does a number have?

Write the prime factorisation n = p^a × q^b × …. The number of positive factors is (a + 1)(b + 1)…. For 360 = 2^3 × 3^2 × 5 that is 4 × 3 × 2 = 24.

What are the factors of 0?

There is no finite list. Every nonzero integer divides 0, so the list would be 1, 2, 3, … without end. Zero is also never a factor of any number.

What are the factors of a negative number?

The positive factors of a negative number are those of its absolute value: −36 has the same positive factors as 36. Signed pairs, whose product is the negative number, are listed separately, for example 4 × (−9) = −36 and (−4) × 9 = −36.

Is 1 a prime number?

No. A prime has exactly two positive factors, and 1 has only one. It is neither prime nor composite.

What is a factor pair?

Two factors whose product is the number, such as 4 and 9 for 36. A number has an equal pair, such as 6 × 6, only when it is a perfect square.

What is the difference between factors, prime factors and prime factorisation?

Factors are all the numbers that divide it exactly (12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12). Prime factors are the factors that are prime (12: 2 and 3). The prime factorisation is the number written as a product of primes (12 = 2^2 × 3).

How large a number can it handle?

Up to 10^18, which is 19 digits, exactly. Larger numbers can have more factors than a page can list and can take very long to factor, so they are refused.

How is this different from the GCF Calculator?

This page lists all the factors of one number. The GCF Calculator finds the largest factor that two or more numbers share.

Is anything sent to a server?

The calculation runs in your browser. The history list is stored only in this browser and you can clear it. This page also loads Google Tag Manager; data collected by tags depends on its configuration.
