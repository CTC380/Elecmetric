How to find a square root and simplify a radical

The square root of x, written √x, is the non-negative number y with y × y = x. When x is a perfect square the answer is a whole number or a fraction. Otherwise the answer is irrational, and the exact value is a radical. To simplify √72, split off the biggest square factor: 72 = 36 × 2, so √72 = 6√2. The working the calculator shows is:

  1. Read the radicand: x = 72.
  2. The principal square root √x is the non-negative number y with y × y = x.
  3. Factorise the radicand: 72 = 2³ × 3². Take out pairs of equal factors: 72 = 6² × 2.
  4. Then √72 = √(6² × 2) = 6 × √2 = 6√2.
  5. The radicand 2 is not a perfect square, so √72 is irrational: its decimal never ends and never repeats. The decimal is an approximation.
  6. Decimal (rounded to 8 places): √72 ≈ 8.48528137.

Rules this calculator follows

  • Principal root. √x is the non-negative root. The equation y² = 9 has two solutions, 3 and −3, but √9 is 3. Solving equations is a different job, for the Quadratic Formula Calculator.
  • Perfect squares first. A number is a perfect square when it has an exact integer square root, found with an integer square root routine on the numerator and the denominator, never from a rounded decimal. So √0.25 is exactly 1/2 and √(9/16) is exactly 3/4.
  • Radicals in simplest form. The largest square factor is taken out, a denominator is rationalised (√(1/2) = √2/2) and the coefficient is reduced.
  • No false exactness. A decimal of an irrational root is marked ≈ and is rounded half up to the places you choose. An exact decimal is shown in full.
  • Negative radicands. A negative number has no real square root. The calculator says so and names the complex value instead of showing a misleading real one.
  • Exact arithmetic. The radicand is an exact fraction in whole-number (BigInt) arithmetic. No floating-point value is used anywhere.
  • Checked every time. The exact result is squared back to the radicand, the decimal is squared against a stated rounding bound, and the root is placed between its two neighbouring whole numbers.

Estimating a square root by hand

Newton’s method, also called the Babylonian or Heron method, estimates √x from a first guess g by repeatedly replacing g with the average of g and x ÷ g. Starting from 9 for √72, each step roughly doubles the number of correct digits:

Questions

How do I find the square root of a number?

The square root of x is the non-negative number that gives x when multiplied by itself. Enter the number and the calculator gives the exact value, the simplified radical and the decimal. For 144 the answer is 12 because 12 × 12 = 144.

How do I simplify a square root?

Write the radicand as a product with the largest perfect square factor, then take that factor’s root outside. 72 = 36 × 2, so √72 = 6√2. A radical is in simplest form when the number under the root has no repeated prime factor.

What is the square root of 2?

√2 is irrational. To 8 decimal places it is 1.41421356. The calculator marks it as an approximation because the decimal never ends and never repeats.

Can I take the square root of a fraction or a decimal?

Yes. A decimal such as 0.25 is held as the exact fraction 1/4 and its root is exactly 1/2. A fraction such as 3/4 gives √3/2, with the denominator rationalised when needed.

Why is there no real square root of a negative number?

A real number multiplied by itself is never negative, so √(−9) has no real result. In complex numbers the principal root is 3i, and the calculator states that value in its message.

What does perfect square mean?

A perfect square is a whole number that is the square of a whole number, such as 1, 4, 9, 16 and 25. A fraction is a perfect square when its numerator and denominator both are, such as 9/16.

Is anything sent to a server?

No. The calculation runs in your browser. The history list is stored only in this browser and you can clear it.
