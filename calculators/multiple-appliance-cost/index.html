Limits of this calculator

A compressor load such as a refrigerator or an air conditioner cycles on and off, so entering its rated power for 24 hours overstates the energy. Enter a measured average power, or reduce the share of its rating drawn. The total is the energy cost of the appliances you listed at one flat rate, not your bill: a real bill adds slab rates, fixed charges, duty and tax. To turn the monthly units into a bill, use Electricity Bill from Tariff Slabs. If the listed total is well below your meter reading, an appliance is missing or the hours are too low.

What this calculator checks

  • The energy of each appliance row (number of units x power of one unit x share of rating drawn x hours per day), the total per day, the monthly energy for the days entered and the yearly energy for 365 days.
  • The cost of each row and of the total at one flat tariff per kWh, each row's share of the total energy, the largest user (the first row on a tie), the connected load of the listed units and the average power over the day.
  • Rejection of a row with no power in every row, of hours above 24 a day, of a negative power and of a fractional number of units; a warning for a row entered at full rating for 24 hours.

What remains unverified

  • The powers, hours and shares of rating themselves, which are your measurements or estimates and decide the result; a cycling load such as a refrigerator or an air conditioner needs a measured average power.
  • Slab or telescopic tariffs, fixed and demand charges, time-of-use rates, power factor penalties, electricity duty and tax; the result is the energy cost at one rate, not a bill. Use Electricity Bill from Tariff Slabs for a bill.
  • Seasonal or weekday patterns, standby power of appliances that are switched off but plugged in, and the difference between the real power and the apparent power of a load.

Multiple Appliance Cost Formula

Row: E per day = n × P × (share / 100) × hours Total Ed = ΣE per day Month: Em = Ed × days Year: Ey = Σ (row E per day × 365 × months of use / 12 + row E per day at the other-month hours × 365 × (12 − months of use) / 12); with a monthly pattern: Ey = Σ rows n × P × share × Σ over months (hours in month × days in month) Cost = energy (kWh) × tariff Share = row energy / total × 100

Assumptions and Limitations

  • Each row is one kind of appliance: n identical units, each drawing the stated power, for the stated hours a day, every day of the period
  • The share of rating is the average fraction of the entered power actually drawn while the appliance is on. Rated power, connected load, average power and maximum demand are different quantities and are not interchangeable
  • One flat tariff per kWh: no slabs, fixed charge, demand charge, time-of-use rate, duty or tax
  • The monthly figure uses the days entered (30 by default) for a month in which every appliance is in use. The yearly figure uses 365 days and, for each appliance, only the months of use per year you enter (12 by default), so twelve monthly figures do not add up to the year unless the days entered are 30.42 and every appliance runs all year
  • Seasonal use has two levels per appliance: the hours a day in its months of use, and (optionally) a lower number of hours a day in the remaining months. Whole months only. Alternatively set the hours pattern of a row to enter hours per day for each of the twelve months; the yearly energy is then the sum of each month’s hours × the days of that calendar month (365 days in all), and the months of use and other-month fields of that row are ignored. The daily and monthly results still use the hours-per-day field

A compressor load such as a refrigerator or an air conditioner cycles on and off, so entering its rated power for 24 hours overstates the energy. Enter a measured average power, or reduce the share of its rating drawn. The total is the energy cost of the appliances you listed at one flat rate, not your bill: a real bill adds slab rates, fixed charges, duty and tax. To turn the monthly units into a bill, use Electricity Bill from Tariff Slabs. If the listed total is well below your meter reading, an appliance is missing or the hours are too low.

What does this calculator assume?

Each row is one kind of appliance: n identical units, each drawing the stated power, for the stated hours a day, every day of the period. The share of rating is the average fraction of the entered power actually drawn while the appliance is on. Rated power, connected load, average power and maximum demand are different quantities and are not interchangeable. One flat tariff per kWh: no slabs, fixed charge, demand charge, time-of-use rate, duty or tax. The monthly figure uses the days entered (30 by default) for a month in which every appliance is in use. The yearly figure uses 365 days and, for each appliance, only the months of use per year you enter (12 by default), so twelve monthly figures do not add up to the year unless the days entered are 30.42 and every appliance runs all year. Seasonal use has two levels per appliance: the hours a day in its months of use, and (optionally) a lower number of hours a day in the remaining months. Whole months only. Alternatively set the hours pattern of a row to enter hours per day for each of the twelve months; the yearly energy is then the sum of each month’s hours × the days of that calendar month (365 days in all), and the months of use and other-month fields of that row are ignored. The daily and monthly results still use the hours-per-day field.
