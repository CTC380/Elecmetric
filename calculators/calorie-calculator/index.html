Three different numbers

REE is the energy your body uses at rest. TDEE adds everything you do during the day. Your calorie target is what you plan to eat, which can sit above or below maintenance depending on your goal. BMR and TDEE are not the same thing.

Prediction equations estimate resting energy. They do not directly measure basal metabolic rate.

Energy at a glance

Method comparison

Differences are expected because each equation uses a different model and different inputs. They are not equally accurate for every person. The selected method is highlighted.

How this was calculated

    The four equations

    Mifflin-St Jeor

    Men: REE = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5 Women: REE = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161 W = weight (kg), H = height (cm), A = age (years)

    Intended for healthy adults. A common default for resting energy. TDEE = REE × activity factor.

    Revised Harris-Benedict

    Men: REE = 13.397W + 4.799H − 5.677A + 88.362 Women: REE = 9.247W + 3.098H − 4.330A + 447.593

    An older equation, revised in 1984. Useful as a second opinion. It was derived from a smaller, older dataset.

    Katch-McArdle

    LBM = W × (1 − body-fat fraction) RDEE = 370 + 21.6 × LBM

    Uses lean body mass, so it is most useful when you have a reliable body-fat estimate. It does not use age or sex.

    2023 DRI Estimated Energy Requirement

    EER = a − (age coefficient × age) + b × height (cm) + c × weight (kg) Separate coefficients for each sex and for four activity categories

    From the National Academies 2023 Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy, for adults 19 and older. It predicts total daily energy directly from doubly labelled water data, so no activity multiplier is applied.

    Safeguards built into this calculator

    • Adults aged 18 to 80 only. The DRI equation starts at 19.
    • Not for pregnancy or breastfeeding. Children and teenagers need separate validated models.
    • No calorie deficit is calculated if BMI is below 18.5.
    • Targets are never set below 1,500 kcal/day for men or 1,200 kcal/day for women. The result tells you when this limit is applied.
    • Warnings appear for deficits above 25% of maintenance, intakes below resting energy, and loss rates above 0.75 kg per week.
    • Body-fat input is checked. Values of 0%, 100% or outside 3% to 60% are rejected.

    Limitations

    • Equations give estimates, not measurements. Individual error can be large even when group-level accuracy is good. For the DRI equations, the average absolute error was about 190 to 270 kcal/day in the source data.
    • Activity multipliers are assumptions. Picking the wrong level is the most common source of error.
    • A fixed calorie deficit does not produce perfectly linear weight loss. Body weight, adaptation and activity all change over time. A dynamic weight model is planned for a later module.
    • The ±10% band shown on maintenance is an indicative range, not a statistical confidence interval.
    • This is not medical advice. Speak to a doctor or registered dietitian before making major changes to your diet, especially with a medical condition, a history of disordered eating, or a large weight goal.
