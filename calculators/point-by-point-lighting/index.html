What this calculator checks

  • The horizontal illuminance at each point of a grid over the area served by a rectangular array of identical luminaires (up to 10 by 10), as the sum over the luminaires of the maintenance factor times I0 cos^m(g) cos(g) / d^2, and the illuminance at a point the user names.
  • The average, minimum and maximum of the grid points, the uniformity Uo = Emin / Eavg, the ratio U1 = Emin / Emax, the position of the darkest point, the luminous flux the intensity pattern implies, the share of it that lands on the area, and the half-intensity angle.
  • A comparison of the average and of Uo with the values the user requires, the lux the darkest point would need, and a warning when there are fewer calculation points than luminaires along a side.

What remains unverified

  • Light reflected from walls, floor and ceiling (only direct light is counted), obstructions, tilted or aimed luminaires, luminaires that differ from one another, and any real candela table: the intensity is the fitted pattern I0 cos^m(g), the same in every plane round the vertical.
  • The required average illuminance and uniformity themselves, and whether the calculation grid, the task area and the area edge follow a standard or specification: they are the user's figures and choices, and no lighting standard is applied.
  • Glare, colour rendering, flicker, the size of the luminaire (each is treated as a point source), the maintenance factor of the installation (it is the user's figure), and any road, sports or floodlighting layout rules.

Point-by-Point Lighting Formula

E at a point = MF × Σ I(γ) × cos γ / d² I(γ) = I0 × cos^m γ cos γ = h / d d² = r² + h² So each luminaire gives MF × I0 × h^(m+1) / (r² + h²)^((m+3)/2), r being its horizontal distance from the point Eavg = mean of the grid points Uo = Emin / Eavg U1 = Emin / Emax Lowest point needed = Uo,required × Eavg Flux of one luminaire Φ = I0 × 2π / (m + 1) Share landing on the area = (Eavg / MF) × area / (n × Φ) Half-intensity angle = arccos(0.5^(1/m))

Assumptions and Limitations

  • Direct light only: nothing reflected from walls, floor or ceiling is added, so the result is lower than a lit room and the area edge is dark because no light comes from beyond it
  • The plane is horizontal and the luminaires hang straight down with no tilt; the illuminance is the horizontal component on that plane
  • Every luminaire is the same and has the same maintenance factor, with intensity I0 × cos^m γ at the angle γ from the vertical, round the vertical axis; a real luminaire with a different pattern in different planes is not matched
  • Each luminaire is a point source: the plane must be farther away than about five times the luminaire size
  • The calculation points are at the centres of equal rectangles over the area the array serves; the average is the plain mean of those points, so a different grid, or points outside the array, will give different averages and a different minimum
  • No obstruction, shadowing, glare, colour or visual-comfort assessment
