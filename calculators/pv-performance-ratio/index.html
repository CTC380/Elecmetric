Limits of this calculator

The performance ratio says how much of the energy the array would make at its nameplate rating, in the sunlight that fell on it, actually reached the meter. A PR of 80 % means 20 % was lost on the way to temperature, dirt, wiring, the inverter, downtime and the like. Because the sunlight is divided out, a good plant and a poor one can be compared across sites and seasons, although temperature still moves it: PR is lower in a hot month, which is why the temperature-corrected PR is shown. The waterfall adds up the losses you expect, and the gap to the measured energy shows what is not explained by them: a measured PR well below the expected one points to a fault, soiling above the figure entered, an outage or a sensor problem, and one above it suggests the losses entered are too high or the irradiation sensor reads low. A new plant often reaches 80 to 85 % and a poor one is below 70 %, but the right figure depends on the climate, mounting and design, so treat those as rough bands and use your contract or design figure for acceptance. This is a worksheet on the figures you enter, not an acceptance test to IEC 61724-1 or IEC 62446-3: the monitoring class, the sensor calibration, the data filtering, the interval-by-interval temperature correction and the test period come from your procedure.

What this calculator checks

  • The IEC 61724-1 energy ratio on the figures the user enters: the final yield Yf = E / P0 from the AC energy at the meter and the nameplate DC power, the reference yield Yr = H / 1 kW/m² from the plane-of-array irradiation, PR = Yf / Yr, the ideal energy P0 × H and the energy lost against it, and the daily final yield.
  • A temperature-corrected PR, PR / (1 + γ (Tm − 25)), from one average module temperature and a linear power coefficient.
  • A seven-step loss waterfall applied one step after another to the energy left by the step before (degradation (1 − d)^years, module temperature, soiling, DC wiring and mismatch, availability and curtailment, other losses, inverter efficiency): the kWh lost at each step, the expected energy, the expected PR (and the PR when new), the gap between the expected and the metered energy in kWh and per cent, and a check against a target PR giving the energy at the target, the shortfall and the margin in points.
  • Warnings for a PR above 100% or above 95%, below 60%, irradiation above 10 kWh/m² per day, a period under 7 days, a corrected PR above 100%, a module colder than 25 °C, a measured energy more than 5% below or 3% above the waterfall, and a PR below the target. Rejections of a positive temperature coefficient, and zero or negative energy, array power, irradiation or days (shared range checks).

What remains unverified

  • Any clause of IEC 61724-1, IEC 61724-2 or IEC 62446-3, or a contract acceptance protocol: the PR is the plain energy ratio on the entered figures, and no licensed text was reviewed. The monitoring class, sensor calibration and uncertainty, data filtering, measurement interval and test period are not checked.
  • The interval-by-interval temperature-weighted correction of IEC 61724-1 (one average module temperature is used), the temperature of the cell as distinct from the module back, and any irradiance-dependent efficiency.
  • Inverter clipping, trackers, bifacial gain, snow, grid availability data, sensor drift, a soiling model and first-year light-induced degradation as a separate step: every loss, the inverter efficiency and the annual degradation are the user's figures, and the order of the waterfall affects the kWh given to each step.

PV Performance Ratio Formula

Yf = E / P0 Yr = H / G_STC with G_STC = 1 kW/m² PR = Yf / Yr = E / (P0 × H) Ideal energy Eid = P0 × H Temperature-corrected PR = PR / (1 + γ (Tm − 25)) Waterfall (each step is taken on the energy left by the one before): degradation (1 − d)^years, temperature 1 + γ (Tm − 25), soiling, DC wiring and mismatch, availability, other, inverter efficiency Expected PR = product of the factors Gap = expected energy − measured energy Shortfall = target PR × Eid − E

  7. Target 80 %: energy at the target = 15,200 kWh; shortfall 200 kWh; PR is -1.05 points below the target

Assumptions and Limitations

  • The performance ratio is the plain IEC 61724-1 energy ratio PR = (E / P0) / (H / 1 kW/m²) over the period you give: the energy is the AC energy at the metering point, P0 is the nameplate DC power at standard test conditions, and H is the plane-of-array irradiation over the same hours; PR is not an efficiency and has no unit
  • The irradiation is measured in the plane of the modules, the energy and irradiation cover exactly the same period, and the sensor is accurate (a pyranometer is usually within 2 to 5 %); a PR of a single day or a week is noisy and one over a whole year is the figure usually used for acceptance
  • The temperature step uses one average module temperature and a linear power coefficient from 25 °C; it is not the interval-by-interval temperature-weighted correction of IEC 61724-1, so the corrected PR is an estimate
  • The waterfall is a design estimate in which every loss is a percentage you enter, applied one after another to the energy left by the step before; the kWh given to each step depends on that order, although the expected PR does not
  • Degradation is compounded each year, (1 − d)^years, from the nameplate power; light-induced degradation in the first year, if the data sheet gives it, is part of the annual figure you enter
  • No inverter clipping, tracker, bifacial gain, snow, grid availability data, sensor drift or soiling model is included: a clipped, bifacial or tracked plant should enter those effects in the other losses (a gain can be entered as a negative figure only by reducing the other losses)

What does this calculator assume?

The performance ratio is the plain IEC 61724-1 energy ratio PR = (E / P0) / (H / 1 kW/m²) over the period you give: the energy is the AC energy at the metering point, P0 is the nameplate DC power at standard test conditions, and H is the plane-of-array irradiation over the same hours; PR is not an efficiency and has no unit.
