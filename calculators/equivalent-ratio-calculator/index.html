How equivalent ratios work

Two ratios are equivalent when one is the other with every term multiplied (or divided) by the same non-zero number. The ratio 2 : 3 and the ratio 4 : 6 describe the same proportion, because 4 : 6 is 2 : 3 with both terms doubled. The working for 2 : 3 is:

  1. Read the two terms in order: 2 : 3. Their simplest form is 2 : 3.
  2. A ratio keeps its value when every term is multiplied (or divided) by the same non-zero number. The terms never change order.
  3. Multiply every term by each multiplier you listed (6 multipliers).
  4. For the multiplier 1: 1 × 2 = 2; 1 × 3 = 3, so 2 : 3 = 2 : 3.

Separate the terms with a colon. A comma is ignored inside a number, so 1,000 is read as 1000. For the multipliers, separate values with commas, or type a whole-number range such as 1-10.

The four ways to scale

  • A table of multiples. Lists the ratio multiplied by each multiplier. The default is 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10, and any positive number, fraction or decimal can be used, so a multiplier of 1/2 gives a smaller ratio.
  • Multiply by a factor (scale up). Multiplies every term by one number you choose and shows the result term by term.
  • Divide by a factor (scale down). Divides every term by one number. The result may have decimals or fractions; the whole-number list below it shows every version that stays in whole numbers.
  • Scale to a target value. Choose a term and the value it should reach. The scale factor is the target divided by that term, and every other term is scaled by the same factor. A term that is 0 cannot be scaled to a target.

Scaling down to whole numbers

Every result also lists the equivalent ratios that stay in whole numbers. They come from dividing the whole-number form of the ratio by each divisor of its greatest common divisor, and the last one is the simplest form. For 12 : 18 the greatest common divisor is 6, so there are four whole-number versions:

For a ratio with decimals or fractions the list starts from its whole-number form, found with the lowest common multiple of the denominators.

Rules this calculator follows

  • One factor for every term. Every term is multiplied by the same number, so the ratio keeps its value. The terms are never sorted: 2 : 3 and 3 : 2 are different ratios.
  • The factor is above zero. A factor of 0 would turn every term into 0, and a negative factor would give negative terms. Both are refused with a message, and dividing by 0 is refused too.
  • Terms are zero or positive. A ratio compares sizes of like quantities, so a negative term is refused, as in the Ratio Simplifier. A zero term stays 0 in every result.
  • Exact arithmetic. Terms and factors are exact fractions held in whole-number (BigInt) arithmetic, with no floating-point rounding. A result is shown as a whole number, an exact decimal when it ends within twelve places, or an exact fraction.
  • Checked every time. Every result is rebuilt from its scale factor, cross-multiplied against the entered ratio and reduced to the simplest form before the page says it is verified.
  • Limits. Two to twelve terms, up to sixty multipliers in a table, and divisors are listed only when the greatest common divisor is at most 1012.

Questions

What is an equivalent ratio?

It is a ratio with the same value as another, found by multiplying or dividing every term by the same non-zero number. 2 : 3, 4 : 6 and 6 : 9 are equivalent.

How do I find equivalent ratios?

Multiply every term by the same number. Enter the ratio, choose A table of multiples, and the calculator lists the results for each multiplier. To scale down, divide every term by a common divisor.

How do I scale a ratio so one term equals a number?

Choose Scale to a target value, pick the term and enter the value. The scale factor is the target divided by that term, and the other terms are multiplied by the same factor. For 3 : 4 with term 2 set to 100 the factor is 25 and the result is 75 : 100.

Can I scale down a ratio?

Yes. Use a factor below 1 (such as 1/2), the divide option, or read the whole-number list, which gives every version of the ratio that stays in whole numbers, ending at the simplest form.

Does it work with three or more terms?

Yes. Enter terms separated by colons, up to twelve. Every term is scaled by the same factor and the order is kept.

Can I use decimals and fractions?

Yes. Each term and factor is read as an exact fraction, so 1.5 : 2.5 scaled by 2 gives exactly 3 : 5 with no rounding. Mixed numbers such as 2 1/2 also work.

Why is a term shown as a fraction?

A term that cannot be written as a decimal with twelve places or fewer, such as one third, is shown as an exact fraction so that nothing is rounded.

Is anything sent to a server?

The calculation runs in your browser. The history list is stored only in this browser and you can clear it. This page also loads Google Tag Manager; data collected by tags depends on its configuration.
