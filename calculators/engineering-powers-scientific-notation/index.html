    The exponent in an RC curve

    Capacitor discharge is V(t) = V0 e−t/RC. With V0 = 100 V, R = 10 kΩ and C = 100 µF, τ = RC = 1 s, so at t = 1 s the exponent is −1 and V = 100 e−1 = 36.79 V. The exponent is simply time measured in time constants.

    How the calculator works

    Take 0.000047. The digits are read exactly, and the decimal point is moved until one non-zero digit stands in front of it. The working below is the calculator’s own.

    Read the number

    0.000047 is read digit by digit as a decimal, without floating point: sign +, digits 47, leading power 10−5.

    Scientific notation

    Move the decimal point until one non-zero digit stands in front of it.
The digits are 47; the first one carries the power 10−5.
That is a move of 5 places to the right.
4.7 × 10−5   (1 ≤ |coefficient| < 10)

    Engineering notation

    Use an exponent that is a multiple of 3, never above the number's own exponent -5: -6.
The decimal point then sits 1 place to the right of the first digit, so the coefficient is 47 (1 ≤ |coefficient| < 1000).
47 × 10−6

    SI prefix

    10−6 = micro (µ), so 47 × 10−6 = 47 µ.

    For an exponential function the working shows the time constant, the exponent and the result:

    The law

    RC discharging:   V(t) = V0 e^(−t/RC)
τ = R C   (the time constant, in seconds)
V0 = the voltage at t = 0, which falls towards zero

    Time constant

    τ = R × C = 10 kΩ × 100 µF
τ = 1 s   (1 × 100 s)

    The exponent

    n = t / τ = 1 s ÷ 1 s = 1
e^(−n) = e^(−1) = 0.3679

    The result

    V(t) = V0 e^(−n) = 100 V × 0.3679
V(t) = 36.79 V   (3.679 × 101 V)

    Rules and limits

    • Numbers may be typed as 4700, 4.7e3, 4.7 x 10^3, 10^-3 or with a prefix letter (4.7k, 100u, 100µ, 2.2M). Letters are case sensitive: m is milli, M is mega. Commas are accepted only as groups of three digits (1,000).
    • Notation, powers of ten and prefix conversion are exact up to 1000 digits and exponents of ±100000.
    • The exponential functions use double-precision arithmetic; time may not exceed 100 000 time constants.
    • RC and RL results are for ideal first-order circuits: no leakage, ESR, source resistance or coil capacitance, and mathematical results only, not a design or safety assessment.
    • The power-of-ten exponent must be a whole number. Fractional exponents are in Mode 02, missing-value problems in Mode 03, rules in Mode 04.

    Questions

    What are scientific notation and engineering notation?

    A number written as a coefficient from 1 up to (but not including) 10, times a power of ten: 4 700 000 = 4.7 × 106 and 0.000047 = 4.7 × 10−5. The exponent counts how many places the decimal point moves. This calculator reads digits exactly, so no digit is lost to floating point. What is engineering notation? Scientific notation with the exponent forced to a multiple of three, so the coefficient is from 1 up to 1000: 0.000047 = 47 × 10−6, and 4 700 000 = 4.7 × 106. The exponents match the SI prefixes (10−6 is micro, 103 is kilo), which is why engineers use it.

    Which SI prefix goes with which power of ten?

    Engineering notation uses the prefixes whose power is a multiple of three, from quecto (10−30) to quetta (1030). The table on this page lists them all. Deci, centi, deca and hecto are SI prefixes too, but they are not used in engineering notation; the SI Prefix conversion mode still accepts them.

    How do I type 4.7 kΩ or 100 µF?

    Type the number with the prefix letter: 4.7k, 100u (or 100µ), 2.2M, 10m. Letters are case sensitive: m is milli and M is mega. You can also type 4.7e3, 4.7E3, 4.7 x 103 or 10−3. Exa is chosen from the prefix list because the letter E means the exponent.

    How is the RC discharge example worked out, and what is a time constant?

    With V0 = 100 V, R = 10 kΩ and C = 100 µF the time constant is τ = RC = 1 s. At t = 1 s, V = 100 × e^(−1) = 36.79 V (to 4 significant figures), which is 36.79 % of the starting voltage. After five time constants only 0.673795 % remains. What is a time constant? The time in which an RC or RL response covers 63.2 % of the way to its final value (charging) or falls to 36.8 % (discharging). RC: τ = R × C. RL: τ = L ÷ R. After five time constants the response is within 0.7 % of its final value.

    Can it find the time to reach a voltage?

    Yes. Enter a target value in the RC and RL modes. Charging from 0 V towards 5 V with R = 1 kΩ and C = 1 µF, it reaches 4 V after 1.60944 ms, from t = −τ ln(1 − V/Vs). A value the curve only approaches, such as the final value itself or zero when discharging, is refused with a reason.

    How accurate is it, and how is rounding done?

    Notation, powers of ten and prefix conversion are exact: digits are handled as text, so a 40-digit number keeps its 40 digits. The exponential functions use double-precision arithmetic with about 15 reliable digits. If a result is outside the double range (more than 700 time constants) it is shown from its logarithm, with about 10 digits, and labelled approximate. How is rounding done? Half up on the digits, once, to the number of significant figures you choose. A carry is renormalised: 999.96 to 4 significant figures is 1.000 × 103, not 1000.0 × 100.

    Does it replace the RC and RL transient calculators?

    No. This mode shows the exponent and the curve for the basic first-order responses. Circuits with a source and initial conditions, or second-order RLC circuits, belong in the RC/RL Transient and RLC Transient calculators, linked below.

    What is the difference between growth, decay and compound growth?

    Growth and decay use the rate k per unit of time with e as the base: y = y0 e^(kt) or y0 e^(−kt). Compound growth repeats a percentage per period: A = P(1 + r/100)^n, which equals e^(n ln(1 + r/100)). The calculator shows the doubling or halving time for each.

    Why is a decimal form sometimes not shown, and can the exponent be a fraction, like 10^2.5?

    A number such as 10500 would need 501 characters. Decimal forms longer than 400 characters are not written out; the notations are still given exactly. Can the exponent be a fraction, like 10^2.5? Not here: the exponent of a power of ten must be a whole number so the answer is exact. For fractional exponents use the Negative and Fractional Exponents mode (Mode 02).

    No. The calculation runs in your browser, and the history list is stored only in your browser.
