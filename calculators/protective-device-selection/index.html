Limits of this calculator

Choosing a protective device is four checks on one circuit. The rating has to carry the load and still protect the cable, which gives a window between the design current and the cable capacity; the device has to be able to break the largest fault; it has to trip on the smallest fault; and it has to let through no more energy than the cable can take. The calculator finds the window and the standard ratings in it and then checks the other three against the figures you enter. It does not check discrimination or back-up protection with other devices, the voltage rating, the number of poles, temperature derating of the device, motor starting or inrush, earth-fault loop impedance and disconnection time, arc-flash energy or any rule of an installation code. Read the device ratings and curves from the maker's data sheet.

What this calculator checks

  • A single circuit with one protective device: the kind of device (miniature, moulded-case or air circuit-breaker, or gG fuse link), the design current Ib, the cable current-carrying capacity Iz, the conventional operating factor k2 of the device, the prospective fault current Isc, the rated breaking capacity of the device to be used, the smallest fault current at the far end, the highest instantaneous trip setting as a multiple of the rating, the let-through energy I²t, the conductor area S and the adiabatic constant k.
  • The two-part overload condition In ≥ Ib and In ≤ Iz with k2 In ≤ 1.45 Iz (so In ≤ min(Iz, 1.45 Iz / k2)): the smallest standard rating at or above Ib from a common catalogue list for the kind of device (miniature 6 to 125 A, moulded-case 16 to 1,600 A, air circuit-breaker 630 to 6,300 A, gG fuse 2 to 1,250 A), the largest rating the cable allows, the loading Ib / In, the conventional operating current k2 In and its ratio to Iz, and whether any rating fits.
  • The smallest standard breaking capacity at or above Isc (3 to 150 kA list) and the ratio of the rated capacity of the device to Isc; the smallest end-of-circuit fault against the highest instantaneous trip m In (not made for a fuse); the cable withstand k² S² against the let-through I²t; and the number of checks not met.
  • Warnings for no standard rating between the load and the cable limit, a breaking capacity below the fault current, a fault current above 150 kA, a smallest fault below the highest trip setting, a let-through above the cable withstand, and a smallest fault larger than the prospective fault. Rejections of a design current above the largest rating in the list for the kind of device and of ranges outside the shared limits (a factor k2 of 1 to 3, positive currents).

What remains unverified

  • Any rule of an installation code or product standard beyond the textbook two-part condition: the 1.45 and k2 figures, the rating lists, the breaking capacity steps, the instantaneous trip bands and the adiabatic constant are conventions or the user's data-sheet figures and are not drawn from a licensed edition of IEC 60364-4-43, IEC 60898-1, IEC 60947-2 or IEC 60269.
  • Discrimination (selectivity) and back-up protection between devices in series, the voltage and pole rating, ambient temperature derating of the device, motor starting and transformer inrush, earth-fault loop impedance and the disconnection time on a time-current curve, arc-flash energy, and the energy let-through curve itself (the I²t is entered, not looked up).
  • Circuits with several devices, adjustable thermal and long-time settings, electronic trip units and short-time delay, direct current, and fuse time-current curves: the check is one rating and one set of fault figures.

  6. Cable withstand k² S² = 115² × 25² = 8,265,625 A²s against a let-through of 400,000 A²s: ratio 20.66

Assumptions and Limitations

  • A single circuit with one protective device against overload and short circuit; the rating is chosen from common catalogue steps for the kind of device (miniature: 6 to 125 A; moulded-case: 16 to 1,600 A; air circuit-breaker: 630 to 6,300 A; gG fuse: 2 to 1,250 A), which differ between makers and must be confirmed against the range you will buy
  • The overload rule is the usual two-part condition: the rating is at least the design current and not above the cable capacity, and the current that makes the device operate in the conventional time does not exceed 1.45 times the cable capacity; the operating factor k2 is your figure from the data sheet
  • The breaking capacity step list (3 to 150 kA) is a set of common catalogue values; the rating of the device you plan to use and the prospective fault current are your figures and the check is rated capacity at or above the fault current
  • The instantaneous trip check compares the smallest end-of-circuit fault current with the highest setting of the trip band (a margin you want on top is not added); it is not made for a fuse
  • The cable withstand uses the adiabatic energy k² S² against the let-through I²t you read from the device curve, for one conductor and one fault, with the constant k that you enter
