Assumptions

  • No light is absorbed or reflected on the way

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert Lux to Candela?

E = I × cos θ / d²; I = E × d² / cos θ; d = √(I × cos θ / E). Worked example: Illuminance (lux) 25, distance from the source 4 m, angle of incidence (°) 0 gives 400 cd.

What do I need to enter?

Enter luminous intensity (cd), illuminance (lux), distance from the source and angle of incidence (°). Leave blank the value you want to find, and the form solves for it. Change the unit of a value with its selector and the result stays the same.

What is the difference between candela and lux?

Candela is the light intensity of a source in one direction. Lux is the light arriving on a surface, and it falls with the square of the distance. The same source gives one quarter of the lux at twice the distance.

When is the inverse-square law valid?

When the source is small compared with the distance, at least about five times the size of the source. Close to a large luminaire it overstates the lux.
