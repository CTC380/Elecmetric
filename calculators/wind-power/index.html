About this calculator

This page calculates swept area, power in the wind and electrical output from rotor diameter, wind speed, power coefficient Cp, air density and 1 more input. Change any value or its unit and the result updates. The steps below show the working for the default values, so you can check each line against your own method.

Limits of this calculator

Power rises with the cube of wind speed, so a small error in the wind speed gives a large error in output. Use the manufacturer power curve for real yield.

Cross-check: 6 reference cases, calculated from textbook formulas coded separately from the engine by the same author, agree with this calculator. This is not an independent review.

Verification status: Indicative

Indicative. Estimation only; further engineering checks are required. Basis: Idealised turbine power with an assumed power coefficient.

Formula

A = π D² / 4 Pwind = ½ ρ A v³ Pout = Pwind × Cp × η

Worked example

InputValue
Rotor diameter90 m
Wind speed10 m/s
Power coefficient Cp0.4
Air density (kg/m³)1.225
Drive-train and generator efficiency0.95
  1. A = π × 90² / 4 = 6,361.7 m²
  2. Pwind = 0.5 × 1.225 × 6,361.7 × 10³ = 3,896.6 kW
  3. Pout = 3,896.6 × 0.4 × 0.95 = 1,480.7 kW

Assumptions

  • Steady wind at hub height
  • Cp is for this wind speed, taken from the power curve
  • Standard sea-level air density 1.225 kg/m³

How it is calculated

Each input is converted to the base unit of its quantity, the formula above is applied in the order shown in the worked example, and each result is shown in the unit you choose. Results are held at full precision and rounded only for display, except where a step selects a standard size, as the worked example shows. A value outside its allowed range is refused with a message instead of giving a result.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to enter?

Enter rotor diameter, wind speed, power coefficient Cp, air density (kg/m³) and drive-train and generator efficiency. Change the unit of a value with its selector and the result stays the same.

What does this calculator assume?

Steady wind at hub height. Cp is for this wind speed, taken from the power curve. Standard sea-level air density 1.225 kg/m³.
