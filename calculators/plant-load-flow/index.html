Limits of this calculator

This is a planning tool for the power that flows from the utility to the loads at the maximum demand. Check that the transformer is not overloaded and keeps room for growth (many designers plan for about 70 to 80 % loading, though your specification governs), that the power factor at the utility meets the tariff or supply agreement, and that the capacitor bank does not push the plant bus to a leading power factor at light load. The demand and coincidence factors are your inputs, and they decide the answer: take them from the project specification, measured load data or a standard, not from the defaults. It does not replace a network study.

Assumptions and Limitations

  • Balanced three-phase plant at one voltage level, one main transformer and one capacitor bank
  • Each group has one demand factor and one power factor; the groups add as complex power, so their power factors combine correctly
  • The coincidence factor scales both the kW and the kvar of the sum of the groups, so the plant power factor is that of the sum
  • Transformer copper loss goes with the square of the load, the iron loss and magnetising current stay constant, and the reactive loss follows from the impedance and no-load current
  • The capacitor bank is fully in service at the maximum demand and the supply voltage is nominal, so a capacitor delivers its rated kvar
  • The extra capacitor figure is worked at the present loading; a larger bank trims the transformer reactive loss slightly, so it errs on the high side
  • Cable and busbar losses, voltage drop, harmonics and motor starting are not modelled

Frequently Asked Questions

What do I need to enter?

Enter plant bus voltage (L-L), utility supply voltage (L-L), main transformer rating, transformer impedance (%), transformer full-load copper loss, transformer no-load (iron) loss, transformer no-load current (%), group 1 connected load (for example process motors), group 1 demand factor, group 1 power factor, group 2 connected load (for example HVAC and compressors), group 2 demand factor, group 2 power factor, group 3 connected load (for example heating), group 3 demand factor, group 3 power factor, group 4 connected load (for example lighting and small power), group 4 demand factor, group 4 power factor, coincidence factor between groups, capacitor bank on the plant bus and target power factor at the utility. Change the unit of a value with its selector and the result stays the same.

What does this calculator assume?

Balanced three-phase plant at one voltage level, one main transformer and one capacitor bank. Each group has one demand factor and one power factor; the groups add as complex power, so their power factors combine correctly. The coincidence factor scales both the kW and the kvar of the sum of the groups, so the plant power factor is that of the sum. Transformer copper loss goes with the square of the load, the iron loss and magnetising current stay constant, and the reactive loss follows from the impedance and no-load current. The capacitor bank is fully in service at the maximum demand and the supply voltage is nominal, so a capacitor delivers its rated kvar. The extra capacitor figure is worked at the present loading; a larger bank trims the transformer reactive loss slightly, so it errs on the high side. Cable and busbar losses, voltage drop, harmonics and motor starting are not modelled.
