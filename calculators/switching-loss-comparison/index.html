Limits of this calculator

Use this to choose between two or three MOSFETs for a switching stage, or to see how a change of frequency moves the loss. Enter the stage conditions once, then each device's on-resistance, edge times and gate charge from its data sheet. The break-even frequency is the quick answer: below it the device with the lower on-resistance has the lower loss, above it the device with the faster edges and lower gate charge does. Check the winner against the thermal limit with the loss inside the device and the heat sink calculator, and add the diode recovery and output capacitance losses from the data sheets for a closer figure. What this calculator checks

  • Conduction loss I squared Rds D, overlap switching loss half V I (tr + tf) fsw and gate drive loss Qg Vgs fsw for two or three devices against independent vectors, including the total, the loss inside the device, the switching and gate share and the best device.
  • The frequency where conduction loss equals switching plus gate loss for each device, and the break-even frequency where two devices have equal total loss, found from the line P = Pc + k fsw and checked by solving the equal-loss condition numerically.
  • The refusal of a zero or negative voltage, current, frequency, gate voltage, on-resistance, edge time or gate charge, a duty cycle outside 0 to 1, a partly filled optional device and edge times that do not fit in the on or off time.

What remains unverified

  • Output capacitance (Coss) loss, diode reverse recovery, ringing, the Miller plateau and the dependence of the real transition time on the gate resistor, load current and bus voltage; the switching loss is the overlap estimate only.
  • Temperature, gate voltage and current dependence of Rds(on), gate charge and edge times, and the data sheet test conditions behind the numbers entered.
  • IGBTs and other devices with a saturation voltage and tail current (the model is resistive conduction, so an IGBT against a MOSFET comparison is outside it).
  • Device ratings and selection (voltage and current margin, avalanche, safe operating area), thermal design from the loss to a junction temperature, and a hard-switched against soft-switched stage.

MOSFET Switching Loss Comparison Formula

Conduction Pc = I² × Rds(on) × D\nSwitching (overlap) Psw = ½ × V × I × (tr + tf) × fsw\nGate drive Pg = Qg × Vgs × fsw\nTotal P = Pc + Psw + Pg; loss inside the device = Pc + Psw\nWrite P = Pc + k × fsw with k = ½ V I (tr + tf) + Qg Vgs. Two devices break even where Pc,x + kx f = Pc,y + ky f, so f = (Pc,x − Pc,y) / (ky − kx) when this is above 0. Conduction equals switching plus gate loss at f = Pc / k

Assumptions and Limitations

  • Each device is run at the same voltage, on-state current, duty cycle, frequency and gate voltage. The conduction loss uses the on-state current as an RMS value, as in the MOSFET Loss Formulas guide, with the on-resistance you enter (use the hot value)
  • The switching loss is the usual hard-switched estimate ½ V I (tr + tf) fsw: the voltage is held at V while the current ramps up over tr, and the current is held at I while the voltage ramps up over tf, so each edge costs ½ V I times its time. It leaves out the output capacitance (Coss) loss, the reverse recovery of the diode, ringing and the effect of the Miller plateau on the real transition time, so it is an estimate of the order of the loss. Use the rise and fall times at your own gate drive and load, not the data sheet test conditions, if you can
  • The gate drive loss Qg Vgs fsw is dissipated mostly in the gate driver and the gate resistor, not in the MOSFET. It is in the total for the efficiency of the stage; the loss inside the device (conduction plus switching) is the one that heats the MOSFET
  • The model is for MOSFETs, whose conduction loss is resistive (I² Rds), including silicon carbide and gallium nitride MOSFETs entered by their Rds(on). An IGBT has a fixed saturation voltage and a turn-off tail current, so its conduction loss and switching loss follow different equations and it is not covered
  • On-resistance, switching times and gate charge are taken as constants; temperature, gate voltage and current dependence of the data sheet values are not modelled. Rds(on) and the switching times are entered as the values you choose to compare
  • The break-even frequency assumes the other conditions stay fixed while the frequency changes. If the device is also changed in the circuit (a different gate resistor, for example) the times change too and the comparison has to be run again

What does this calculator assume?

Each device is run at the same voltage, on-state current, duty cycle, frequency and gate voltage. The conduction loss uses the on-state current as an RMS value, as in the MOSFET Loss Formulas guide, with the on-resistance you enter (use the hot value). The switching loss is the usual hard-switched estimate ½ V I (tr + tf) fsw: the voltage is held at V while the current ramps up over tr, and the current is held at I while the voltage ramps up over tf, so each edge costs ½ V I times its time. It leaves out the output capacitance (Coss) loss, the reverse recovery of the diode, ringing and the effect of the Miller plateau on the real transition time, so it is an estimate of the order of the loss. Use the rise and fall times at your own gate drive and load, not the data sheet test conditions, if you can. The gate drive loss Qg Vgs fsw is dissipated mostly in the gate driver and the gate resistor, not in the MOSFET. It is in the total for the efficiency of the stage; the loss inside the device (conduction plus switching) is the one that heats the MOSFET. The model is for MOSFETs, whose conduction loss is resistive (I² Rds), including silicon carbide and gallium nitride MOSFETs entered by their Rds(on). An IGBT has a fixed saturation voltage and a turn-off tail current, so its conduction loss and switching loss follow different equations and it is not covered. On-resistance, switching times and gate charge are taken as constants; temperature, gate voltage and current dependence of the data sheet values are not modelled. Rds(on) and the switching times are entered as the values you choose to compare. The break-even frequency assumes the other conditions stay fixed while the frequency changes. If the device is also changed in the circuit (a different gate resistor, for example) the times change too and the comparison has to be run again.
