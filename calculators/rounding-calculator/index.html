How to round to decimal places

To round a number to a given number of decimal places, keep the digits down to that place and look at the digits after it. If they are more than half of one unit in the last kept place, the kept part goes up by one unit. If they are less than half, it stays. If they are exactly half, the rounding rule decides. Here is 15.496 rounded to two places with the half away from zero rule:

  1. Read the number exactly: 15.496, with 3 decimal places. It is held as an exact decimal, not as a binary floating-point value.
  2. Round to 2 decimal places: keep the digits down to the hundredths place and look at what is dropped. One unit in that place is 0.01.
  3. Kept part: 15.49. Dropped part: 0.006.
  4. Compare the dropped part with half a unit, 0.005: it is more than half, so the size goes up by one unit.
  5. Add one unit: 15.49 + 0.01 = 15.50.
  6. Put the sign back: the result is 15.50.
  7. Absolute rounding difference: |15.50 − 15.496| = 0.004.

A negative number is handled by its size: −15.496 rounds to −15.50, the same size with the sign put back. Only the rule changes what happens on an exact tie, and for the directional rules (ceiling, floor, truncate) the sign decides which way anything dropped moves the value.

Negative decimal places: tens, hundreds and thousands

A negative number of decimal places rounds to the left of the decimal point: −1 is the nearest ten, −2 the nearest hundred and −3 the nearest thousand. Zero decimal places is the nearest whole number.

Rounding rules and how they treat a tie

Most digits are not a tie, and every nearest-value rule gives the same answer for them. A tie is a number exactly halfway between two candidates, such as 2.5 to a whole number. Each rule names exactly where a tie goes, so there is no ambiguity.

Rounding rule2.5−2.53.5How a tie is settled
Half away from zero3−34a tie goes to the larger size: 2.5 → 3 and −2.5 → −3
Half towards zero2−23a tie goes to the smaller size: 2.5 → 2 and −2.5 → −2
Half to even (banker’s rounding)2−24a tie goes to the neighbour whose last kept digit is even: 2.5 → 2 and 3.5 → 4
Half to odd3−33a tie goes to the neighbour whose last kept digit is odd: 2.5 → 3 and 3.5 → 3
Half towards +∞3−24a tie goes to the larger value: 2.5 → 3 and −2.5 → −2
Half towards −∞2−33a tie goes to the smaller value: 2.5 → 2 and −2.5 → −3
Ceiling (round up towards +∞)3−24anything dropped moves the value up: 2.1 → 3 and −2.1 → −2
Floor (round down towards −∞)2−33anything dropped moves the value down: 2.9 → 2 and −2.1 → −3
Truncate (towards zero)2−23the dropped digits are cut off: 2.9 → 2 and −2.9 → −2

Half away from zero is the rule most people learn at school: 2.5 → 3 and −2.5 → −3. Half to even (banker’s rounding) sends ties to the even neighbour, so errors cancel over many values and it is the default in several programming languages. The name “half up” is not used here because it means different things: some calculators, including Calculator.net, use it for half towards +∞, where −5.5 becomes −5, while many people read it as half away from zero. Choose the explicit name that you mean.

Why this calculator is exact

Many calculators and programming languages turn what you type into a binary floating-point number first, and most decimal fractions cannot be stored exactly in binary. The number 2.675 is stored as 2.67499999999999982236431605997495353221893310546875, which is just below 2.675, so a binary calculator rounds it to 2.67. The decimal number 2.675 is exactly halfway between 2.67 and 2.68 and rounds to 2.68 with half away from zero.

This calculator reads your text as an exact decimal and rounds with whole-number (BigInt) arithmetic. It never uses Math.round or toFixed to produce a result, and it handles up to 1000 digits without losing any. The page compares against plain floating point and tells you when the two disagree.

Rounding in electrical engineering reports

A measured voltage of 230.4567 V can be reported at different precisions:

Reported toResult
0 decimal places230 V
1 decimal place230.5 V
2 decimal places230.46 V
3 decimal places230.457 V

Round only the value you report or display, and keep full precision in every intermediate step of a calculation. Rounding a voltage drop or a current part-way through a calculation and then using the rounded value can shift the final answer by more than the rounding you intended.

Rounding is not selection. A calculated cable size, transformer capacity or equipment rating must not be rounded to a convenient number and called a standard size. The selected rating has to be checked against the available standard sizes, the applicable requirements and the conditions of the installation. A reported value should also not show more digits than the measurement supports: how many digits are meaningful is a question of significant figures and measurement uncertainty, covered in the related tools below.

Questions

How do I round a number to 2 decimal places?

Keep two digits after the decimal point and look at the third. If it is 5 or more (with other digits after it) the second digit goes up by one; if it is less than 5 it stays. 15.496 becomes 15.50 and 15.494 becomes 15.49. An exact 5 with nothing after it is a tie, and the rounding rule decides.

What is half away from zero rounding?

It is the common school rule: a tie goes to the neighbour with the larger size. 2.5 becomes 3 and −2.5 becomes −3.

What is banker’s rounding?

Banker’s rounding, also called half to even, sends a tie to the neighbour whose last kept digit is even: 2.5 becomes 2 and 3.5 becomes 4. Because ties go up as often as down, the average error over many values is close to zero.

What is the difference between round half up and half away from zero?

They agree for positive numbers and differ for negative ties. Half away from zero sends −2.5 to −3. Half towards +∞, which some calculators call half up, sends −2.5 to −2. This calculator names each rule in full so that you can pick the one you mean.

How do I round to the nearest ten, hundred or thousand?

Use a negative number of decimal places: −1 for tens, −2 for hundreds, −3 for thousands. 1,499 to the nearest hundred (−2) is 1,500 and to the nearest thousand (−3) is 1,000.

What is the difference between rounding and truncating?

Truncating cuts off the digits after the chosen place and never changes the kept digits, so it always moves a number towards zero: 2.99 truncates to 2. Rounding to the nearest moves to the closer neighbour, so 2.99 rounds to 3.

What do ceiling and floor do to negative numbers?

Ceiling always moves up towards +∞, so −2.1 becomes −2. Floor always moves down towards −∞, so −2.1 becomes −3. Both leave a number unchanged if it is already exact at the chosen place.

Why does my calculator give 2.67 for 2.675?

Because it stores 2.675 in binary floating point as a value just below 2.675. This calculator rounds the exact decimal, so with half away from zero the result is 2.68.

Why does the result keep trailing zeros?

Rounding 15.496 to two places gives 15.50, and the zero shows that the value is given to the hundredths. You can switch the option off to show 15.5. The value is the same.

Is anything sent to a server?

No. The calculation runs in your browser. The history list is stored only in this browser and you can clear it.
