    Questions

    What are the bitwise operators?

    A bitwise operator works on each pair of bits in the same column independently. AND gives 1 only when both bits are 1, OR gives 1 when either is 1, XOR gives 1 when they differ, and NOT flips a bit. NAND, NOR and XNOR are AND, OR and XOR followed by NOT. The grid above shows every column, so you can see which bits produced each result bit.

    Why does NOT need a bit width?

    NOT flips every bit of the register, so the answer depends on how many bits it has. NOT 00001111 is 11110000 in 8 bits but 1111111111110000 in 16 bits. The same is true of shifts, where bits can fall off the end. NOT and the shifts therefore need an explicit width of 4, 8, 16, 32 or 64 bits.

    What is the difference between a logical and an arithmetic right shift?

    A logical right shift shifts zeros in on the left. An arithmetic right shift copies the sign bit in, which keeps a negative two’s complement number negative: 10110000 gives 00101100 logically but 11101100 arithmetically. For a value whose sign bit is 0 they give the same result. This page never mixes them up, and tells you when they differ for your input.

    What happens to the bits that are shifted out?

    They are lost, as in a real register. The page shows them, and a left shift reports unsigned overflow when a 1 is shifted out (the result is no longer A × 2^n) and signed overflow when the two’s complement result no longer fits.

    How do I set, clear, toggle or test one bit?

    Build a mask with a single 1 at the bit position (bit 0 is the rightmost). OR with the mask sets the bit, AND with the inverted mask clears it, XOR with the mask toggles it, and AND with the mask tells you whether the bit is set. The register tools do this for you and show the mask.

    What does Compare do?

    It XORs the two values: every 1 in the result is a bit where they differ. The number of differing bits is the Hamming distance, and their positions are listed.

    What happens if a value is wider than the width I chose?

    It is refused with a message, never truncated. A negative decimal value is encoded as two’s complement and must fit the width, so −129 is refused at 8 bits. A sign is not accepted on a binary, octal or hexadecimal pattern.

    Does this replace the Binary Calculator and Logic Gates page?

    That page does fixed-width arithmetic, overflow reporting and the same logic operations on single values. This page concentrates on seeing the bits: the column grid, the shift sources, the register tools and the explicit logical and arithmetic shifts.

    Is anything sent to a server?

    Everything runs in your browser, and the history list is stored only in your browser. This page also loads Google Tag Manager; data collected by tags depends on its configuration.
