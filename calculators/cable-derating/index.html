About this calculator

Use this to find the current a cable can actually carry once the ambient temperature and grouping are accounted for, or the tabulated ampacity a cable must have to be protected by a given device. It also checks the design current against the device rating and the derated capacity.

Limits of this calculator

Tabulated ampacities are not built in, because they depend on the installation method and come from licensed standards. Enter the value from your cable data sheet or the standard. The calculation bases apply the factors listed in the assumptions below; they are not complete regional table lookups, so check the result against the standard you work to.

Cross-check: 30 reference cases, calculated from textbook formulas coded separately from the engine by the same author, agree with this calculator. This is not an independent review.

Verification status: Indicative

Indicative. Estimation only; further engineering checks are required. Basis: Temperature factor is a heat-balance approximation, not the tabulated standard factor.

What this calculator checks

  • Iz = tabulated rating you enter × temperature factor × grouping factor × other factor.
  • Rule Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz for the chosen basis (1.25 × Ib for the NEC-style continuous case).

What remains unverified

  • The temperature factor is a heat-balance approximation, not the factor tabulated in the wiring standard.
  • The tabulated rating, grouping factor and installation method are yours to take from the data sheet or licensed standard.
  • Harmonics, soil thermal resistivity, voltage drop and short-circuit withstand.

Formula

kt = √((θmax − Ta) / (θmax − Tref)) Iz = Itab × kt × kg × ko Itab,req = In / (kt × kg × ko)

Grouping factor0.8
Other correction factor1
  1. θmax = 90 °C
  2. kt = √((90 − 40) / (90 − 30)) = 0.9129
  3. Combined factor = 0.9129 × 0.8 × 1 = 0.7303
  4. Iz = 300 × 0.7303 = 219.09 A
  5. Required tabulated ampacity = 200 / 0.7303 = 273.86 A

Assumptions

  • Approximate temperature factor from a steady-state conductor heat-balance model; it is not a standard-table correction factor
  • Tabulated ampacity and grouping factor supplied by the user
  • Rule checked: cf × Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz, with cf set by the chosen basis (1.0 General, 1.25 NEC-style continuous)

The temperature multiplier is an engineering approximation, not a substitute for the ambient-temperature correction factor in the applicable wiring standard or cable-manufacturer tables. For final cable selection, use the prescribed factor for the actual cable type, installation method, ambient conditions and grouping. Tabulated ampacity values are not built in because they depend on installation method and may be protected data; read them from your data sheet or licensed standard.

How it is calculated

Each input is converted to the base unit of its quantity, the formula above is applied in the order shown in the worked example, and each result is shown in the unit you choose. Results are held at full precision and rounded only for display, except where a step selects a standard size, as the worked example shows. A value outside its allowed range is refused with a message instead of giving a result.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to enter?

Enter design current, protective device rating, tabulated ampacity (data sheet, reference conditions), calculation basis, insulation, ambient temperature (°C) and grouping factor. Change the unit of a value with its selector and the result stays the same.

Where do I get the tabulated ampacity?

From the cable manufacturer data sheet or the installation table in the standard you work to, for your installation method and conductor. Elecmetric applies the correction factors to that figure; it does not look it up.

How is the temperature factor worked out?

It comes from the heat balance of the conductor, the square root of the margin between the conductor limit and the ambient, over the margin between the conductor limit and the reference ambient of the table. The formula above shows it.

Is cable ampacity a separate calculation from derating?

No. The derated current capacity is the ampacity for your conditions, so this one page covers both questions.

How accurate are the results?

Every figure comes from the same tested engine as the Elecmetric calculators and is meant for estimating. 30 reference cases, calculated from textbook formulas coded separately from the engine by the same author, agree with this calculator.
