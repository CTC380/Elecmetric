Limits of this calculator

Diversity is the main judgement in a charging site. A workplace or home car park where cars sit for hours behaves very differently from a motorway hub where fast chargers are full all day, so test the share in use and the average power against a count from a similar site rather than taking one figure for all of them. Where the supply cannot carry every charger at full rating, load management shares the available kW between the vehicles in use; the last two results show how much it would have to cut. The calculator does not apply a code diversity or demand table (IEC 60364-7-722, NEC Article 625 and local rules differ), nor a utility connection rule: check those separately before ordering equipment.

What this calculator checks

  • The group arithmetic on the figures the user enters: for each of two charger groups the connected load number × rated power / efficiency, the chargers in use as the share in use rounded up to a whole charger, and the demand in use × rated power × average power share / efficiency; the group and total connected load, the charger demand and the diversity factor (demand over connected load).
  • Power factor handled as P and Q for the chargers and for the building load (Q = P tan(arccos pf)): site kW, kvar, kVA, power factor and current (S / (√3 V) three-phase, S / V single-phase) at the peak, and the same with the spare-capacity percentage as the design demand and design current.
  • The next standard transformer at or above the design demand from the common size list (25 to 3,150 kVA) and the peak loading of it.
  • An optional supply limit in kVA: room under it at the peak and at the design demand, the charger kW the limit leaves after the building load (the root of √((P + Pb)² + (P tan φ + Qb)²) = limit), and the share of its rating each charger gets if every charger is used at once at full rating.
  • Warnings for a peak or design demand above the supply limit, every charger at once above what the limit leaves, the building load alone reaching the limit, a share in use below 40%, a charger power factor below 0.9, a large single-phase current, and a design demand above the largest standard transformer. Rejections of no charger in either group, a fractional number of chargers, a zero rating for a group with chargers, and values outside the shared ranges.

What remains unverified

  • Any clause of IEC 60364-7-722, IEC 61851-1, IEC 61439-7, NEC Article 625 or a local rule on EV supply demand, diversity or load management: the share in use, the average power and the efficiency are the user's judgement and no code demand or diversity table is applied. No licensed clause text was reviewed.
  • A utility connection rule, a maximum-demand tariff, a charge-session or arrival model, a load profile over the day, time-of-use control or an energy cost: demand is taken at one busiest moment and the building maximum demand is added in full.
  • Load-management or smart-charging controller design, the per-vehicle taper, vehicles that take less than their share, vehicle-to-grid export, harmonics, phase unbalance, inrush, voltage drop and cable or protection sizing for each charger.

EV Charger Load Formula

Group demand = (chargers in use) × (rated power × average power %) / efficiency chargers in use = round up(number × share in use %) Connected load = Σ number × rated power / efficiency Diversity factor = demand / connected load Q = P × tan(arccos pf) for chargers and for the building S = √((Pc + Pb)² + (Qc + Qb)²) I = S / (√3 × V) (three-phase) or S / V (single-phase) Design S = S × (1 + spare %) Transformer = smallest standard size ≥ design S Power left for chargers: solve √((P + Pb)² + (P tan φc + Qb)²) = limit for P Share = P / connected load

Assumptions and Limitations

  • The two groups are all the chargers on the supply, every charger in a group is the same, and the share in use, the average power while charging and the efficiency are your inputs: the calculator applies a diversity you choose and does not take one from a standard or a code table
  • Demand is taken at one busiest moment, with the chargers and the building load peaking together (the building maximum demand is added in full, which is conservative if they peak at different times)
  • Efficiency is the rated output over the power drawn from the supply at full power; the same figure is used at part load, and a charger standing idle is taken as drawing nothing
  • The power factor of each part is constant, and the chargers are balanced across the three phases; no harmonics, unbalance, inrush or voltage drop is included
  • The supply limit is compared in kVA with the peak and design demand; the load-management figures are the kW and the share of rating that the limit leaves for the chargers after the building load, not a controller design

How to Calculate EV Charger Load

Each input is converted to the base unit of its quantity, the formula above is applied in the order shown in the worked example, and each result is shown in the unit you choose. Results are held at full precision and rounded only for display, except where a step selects a standard size, as the worked example shows. A value outside its allowed range is refused with a message instead of giving a result.
