Limits of this calculator

An uncertainty budget lists everything that could make the result differ from the true value, puts each on the same footing as a standard uncertainty and combines them. Because independent errors seldom all reach their limits together, they are added as squares, so one large source usually dominates: the share column shows which one, and improving a small source will do little. The expanded uncertainty U is the range around the result that is expected to hold the true value with the chosen coverage; quote it with its coverage factor. With few repeated readings the scatter is itself uncertain, which is what the effective degrees of freedom and the Student t factor allow for. The test uncertainty ratio compares that range with the tolerance being checked; 4 to 1 is a common guideline and below 1 means the measurement cannot show whether the quantity is inside the tolerance. This is a worksheet for the standard method, not a calibration laboratory budget: it has no correlation terms, no unknown distributions beyond the six listed and no certificate-specific coverage factors. The correction for known systematic errors, the traceability of the reference and the decision rule come from your procedure.

What this calculator checks

  • One measurement with a result that is a single reading or the mean of n readings: the measured value x, the sample standard deviation s of the readings and their number n (the Type A source, uA = s / √n with n − 1 degrees of freedom), up to four other sources each given as a standard uncertainty, a rectangular, triangular or U-shaped limit, an expanded uncertainty at k = 2 or a resolution step, and a sensitivity coefficient for each.
  • Each source converted to a standard uncertainty by its divisor (1, √3, √6, √2, 2 or 2√3), weighted by |c|, and combined as a root sum of squares into the combined standard uncertainty; the share of each source in the variance and the largest contributor.
  • The effective degrees of freedom by the Welch–Satterthwaite formula with the Type B sources taken as infinite, truncated to a whole number (one million or more taken as infinite); the coverage factor k = 2, k = 3 or a Student t factor for 95% or 99%; the expanded uncertainty U = k uc, the interval x ± U, U as a percentage of the reading and the test uncertainty ratio T / U.
  • Warnings for one source supplying 80% or more of the variance, a standard deviation from fewer than 3 readings, k = 2 or 3 with fewer than 30 effective degrees of freedom, a test uncertainty ratio below 4 and an expanded uncertainty above the tolerance. Rejections of a fractional number of readings, a standard deviation with fewer than 2 readings, a budget with no source, and a negative source value.

What remains unverified

  • Any clause of JCGM 100 (the GUM), JCGM 101, ISO/IEC 17025 or a laboratory accreditation rule: the method is the textbook root-sum-square budget on the figures the user enters, and no licensed text was reviewed.
  • Correlation between sources, a measurand that is a non-linear function of several inputs (the sensitivity coefficients are entered, not derived), Type B sources with a finite degrees of freedom, distributions other than the six listed, and the Monte Carlo method.
  • Correction of known systematic errors (the value x is taken as already corrected), the choice of the sources and the size of the figures you enter, traceability of the reference, a guard band or decision rule for a pass or fail against the tolerance, and the reporting rules of a calibration certificate.

Measurement Uncertainty Formula

Standard uncertainty u: rectangular a / √3 triangular a / √6 arcsine a / √2 resolution d / (2√3) expanded U / 2 Type A: uA = s / √n Contribution qi = |ci| ui Combined uc = √(uA² + q1² + q2² + q3² + q4²) Share of source i = qi² / uc² Effective degrees of freedom (Welch–Satterthwaite): νeff = uc⁴ / (uA⁴ / (n − 1)), the other sources taken as infinite Expanded U = k uc TUR = T / U

Assumptions and Limitations

  • One measurand and a result that is a single reading or the mean of n readings; every source is independent of the others (no correlation terms) and acts on the result through its sensitivity coefficient, with all figures in the unit of the result after the coefficient is applied
  • The distribution of each Type B source is the one you pick: a data-sheet limit is rectangular unless you know better, a limit that the error sits near the ends of is U-shaped, and a limit that the error clusters in the middle of is triangular; a calibration certificate figure is taken as the expanded uncertainty at k = 2 (use a standard uncertainty entry for any other k)
  • The Type A source has n − 1 degrees of freedom and every Type B source is taken as infinite (a Type B figure is treated as exactly known); with no Type A source the effective degrees of freedom are infinite and k is the normal value, 1.960 for 95% and 2.576 for 99%
  • The effective degrees of freedom are truncated to a whole number before the t factor is found, as the GUM advises, and a value of one million or more is treated as infinite
  • The result is the interval x ± U for the stated coverage; it assumes the result is a linear combination of the sources and the combined distribution is close to normal or to Student t, and the correction for any known systematic error is already applied to x
  • The test uncertainty ratio is the plain ratio T / U of the tolerance to the expanded uncertainty; no guard band or decision rule on pass or fail is applied

How to Calculate Measurement Uncertainty

Each input is converted to the base unit of its quantity, the formula above is applied in the order shown in the worked example, and each result is shown in the unit you choose. Results are held at full precision and rounded only for display, except where a step selects a standard size, as the worked example shows. A value outside its allowed range is refused with a message instead of giving a result.
