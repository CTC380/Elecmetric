Dynamic model vs the 7,700 kcal/kg rule

The fixed rule assumes your expenditure stays constant and every kilogram is pure fat. In reality your expenditure falls as you get lighter, and part of the change is lean tissue.

How this was calculated

    How the dynamic model works

    The model runs day by day. On each day:

    1. Expenditure E = Mifflin-St Jeor(current weight) × activity factor 2. Energy balance EB = intake − E 3. Energy density of change ρ = (9400·FM + 1800·10.4) ÷ (FM + 10.4) kcal/kg 4. Weight change ΔW = EB ÷ ρ 5. Fat share of change ΔFM = ΔW × FM ÷ (FM + 10.4) FM = fat mass (kg). 9400 kcal/kg for fat, 1800 kcal/kg for lean tissue.

    The fat/lean split follows Forbes, in which lean mass changes with the logarithm of fat mass. People with more body fat lose a larger share of their weight change as fat, so a kilogram is worth more energy at higher body fat. At 20 kg of fat mass the model uses about 6,800 kcal per kg. At 30 kg it is about 7,400 kcal per kg.

    When you ask for a goal weight, the calculator searches for the constant daily intake that lands on that weight at the end of your chosen period.

    Safeguards

    • Adults 18 to 80. Not for pregnancy or breastfeeding. Children and teenagers need separate validated models.
    • Goal weights that give a BMI under 18.5 are refused. No loss plan is built if your current BMI is under 18.5.
    • Intake is never planned below 1,500 kcal/day for men or 1,200 for women. If your goal would need less, the plan uses the minimum and shows where you would actually get to.
    • Weight-gain surpluses are capped at +1,000 kcal/day.
    • Warnings appear for changes above 0.75 kg per week of loss, more than 1 kg per week in either direction, deficits above 25%, and intakes below resting energy.
    • The plan does not promise an exact date or amount of weight change.

    Limitations

    • This is a simplified model. It is not the NIDDK Body Weight Planner, which models more detail, including changes in body water and adaptation. Compare with that tool for a second opinion.
    • Metabolic adaptation (a drop in expenditure beyond what weight loss explains) is not modelled, so real loss may be slower than shown.
    • Expenditure is recalculated from weight only. Changes in your activity, sleep, illness, medication and water balance are not included.
    • If body fat is estimated rather than measured, the fat share of weight change is uncertain. The estimate comes from BMI, age and sex (Deurenberg et al.), with a typical error of about 4 to 5 percentage points.
    • A constant daily intake is assumed. Real eating varies day to day.
    • This is not medical advice. Speak to a doctor or dietitian before a large change, especially with a medical condition or a history of disordered eating.
