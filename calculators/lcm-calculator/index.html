How to find the LCM of several numbers

The least common multiple of a list of numbers is the smallest positive number that every one of them divides exactly. For two numbers, LCM(a, b) = a ÷ GCD(a, b) × b. For more numbers the calculator repeats this: it finds the LCM of the first two, then the LCM of that result with the third, and so on. For 12, 18 and 24 the working is:

  1. Use LCM(a, b) = a ÷ GCD(a, b) × b (dividing first keeps the numbers small), and fold it over the list: the LCM of the first two numbers, then that result with the third, and so on.
  2. Step 1: LCM(12, 18). Euclidean algorithm: 18 = 1 × 12 + 6; 12 = 2 × 6 + 0. GCD = 6. LCM = 12 ÷ 6 × 18 = 36.
  3. Step 2: LCM(36, 24). Euclidean algorithm: 36 = 1 × 24 + 12; 24 = 2 × 12 + 0. GCD = 12. LCM = 36 ÷ 12 × 24 = 72.

Three ways to find an LCM

MethodWorking for 12 and 18LCM
Listing multiplesMultiples of 12: 12, 24, 36. Multiples of 18: 18, 36.36
Prime factorisation12 = 2² × 3. 18 = 2 × 3². LCM = 2² × 3² = 36.36
GCD methodGCD(12, 18) = 6, so LCM = 12 ÷ 6 × 18 = 36.36

All three give the same answer. Listing multiples is the easiest to see but only works for small answers. Prime factorisation shows the structure of the numbers. The GCD method is the one the calculator uses to compute the result, because it stays fast for very large numbers, where listing multiples or factorising would not finish. Choose “Working shown” to see any of the three. A prime factorisation is shown only for numbers up to 1,000,000,000,000, and listing only when the answer is reached within 40 multiples of the smallest number; outside that the page shows the GCD working and says so.

Rules this calculator follows

  • Whole numbers only. The LCM is defined for integers. A decimal such as 2.5 or a fraction such as 1/2 is refused with a message, while 12.0 and 1e3 are accepted because they are whole numbers. A common denominator for fractions is a different calculation, the Fraction LCM mode of this calculator, which is still to come.
  • The sign is ignored. LCM(−4, 6) = LCM(4, 6) = 12. The answer is never negative.
  • Zero. If any number is 0 the LCM is 0, because the only multiple of 0 is 0. The page states this convention and also shows the LCM of the other numbers.
  • Repeated numbers are used once. A repeated number cannot change the LCM. A single number is its own LCM.
  • Exact arithmetic. Every number is held as a whole number in BigInt arithmetic, so there is no floating-point rounding and no loss of digits, however large the numbers are. The page divides before it multiplies, so the intermediate numbers never grow faster than the answer.
  • Checked every time. The result is checked by routes that do not use the calculation itself: divisibility by every number, the quotients having a GCD of 1 (which proves that no smaller common multiple exists), the reverse order, the GCD, and the highest powers of the primes when the numbers are small enough to factorise.

These examples assume that every event starts at the same instant and that each period is exact. Real timers drift, start at different phases and have jitter, so an LCM gives the repeat interval of an ideal pattern, not a guarantee for a real system. Periods must be written as whole numbers of one common unit before the LCM is taken. For frequencies or fractions, convert to periods or to a common denominator first.

LCM and GCD

The LCM and the greatest common divisor are linked. For two positive numbers, GCD(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b, so for 12 and 18 the product 6 × 36 = 216 equals 12 × 18. This calculator shows the GCD beside every LCM. For three or more numbers the product of the GCD and the LCM is not the product of the numbers, so use the repeated two-number step instead.

Questions

How do I find the LCM of three numbers?

Find the LCM of the first two, then find the LCM of that result and the third. For 12, 18 and 24, LCM(12, 18) = 36 and LCM(36, 24) = 72. Enter the three numbers separated by commas and the calculator shows each step.

How do I find the LCM using prime factors?

Write each number as a product of primes and take the highest power of every prime that appears. For 12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3², the highest powers are 2² and 3², so the LCM is 4 × 9 = 36. Choose Prime factorisation under Working shown.

What is the LCM of numbers that include 0?

By the usual convention it is 0, because the only multiple of 0 is 0. The page shows the LCM of the other numbers as well, since that is usually the figure wanted.

Can I find the LCM of negative numbers?

Yes. The sign is ignored, so LCM(−4, 6) is 12. The least common multiple is always a positive number or 0.

Can I use decimals or fractions?

Not here. An LCM is defined for whole numbers, so 2.5 and 1/2 are refused with a message. A value such as 12.0 or 1e3 is a whole number and is accepted. For a common denominator of fractions use the Fraction tools.

How large can the numbers be?

Each number can have up to 100 digits and up to 50 numbers can be used. The arithmetic is exact, so the answer is not rounded, however long it is.
