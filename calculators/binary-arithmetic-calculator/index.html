Binary Calculator: Add, Subtract, Multiply and Divide

Enter binary numbers of any length and add, subtract, multiply or divide them. Use more than two numbers, negative values and leading zeros; division shows the quotient and the remainder. Every operand and result is shown in binary, decimal, hexadecimal and octal, with the carries, borrows, partial products and long division written out. Calculate. Convert. Understand.

    Questions

    How do you add binary numbers?

    Work from the right, one column at a time. 0 + 0 = 0, 0 + 1 = 1, 1 + 1 = 10 (write 0 and carry 1) and 1 + 1 + 1 = 11 (write 1 and carry 1). The calculator shows the carry row above each column, so 1101 + 1011 = 11000 can be followed digit by digit.

    How does binary subtraction work, and what if the answer is negative?

    Subtract column by column from the right. When a column needs 0 − 1, borrow 1 from the next column to the left. If B is larger than A, the calculator subtracts the smaller number from the larger and puts a minus sign in front, so 101 − 1100 = −111 (5 − 12 = −7).

    How is binary multiplication done?

    Each bit of the multiplier gives one partial product: the multiplicand if the bit is 1, or zero if the bit is 0, shifted one place further left for each bit. The partial products are then added. The working table shows every row.

    What happens to the remainder in binary division?

    It is reported, not discarded. 110101 ÷ 101 gives quotient 1010 and remainder 11 (53 ÷ 5 = 10 remainder 3), and the check Q × B + R = A is shown. Division truncates toward zero, and the remainder takes the sign of the dividend.

    How large can the numbers be?

    Up to 4096 binary digits each, with up to 20 numbers for addition, subtraction and multiplication. The arithmetic is exact (arbitrary precision), so a very long number never overflows or loses digits. Division takes exactly two numbers.

    Do leading zeros matter?

    Not for the value: 0101 and 101 are both 5. They matter only for display. Choose “Match the longest number entered” to keep a fixed number of digits (1010 + 0101 = 1111, 1100 − 0101 = 0111) or a minimum of 4 to 64 bits. A result that needs more bits is always shown in full; nothing is cut off.

    How are negative numbers written?

    With a minus sign in front of the magnitude (sign and magnitude), for example −101 is −5. This is not the same as a fixed-width two’s complement pattern, where the same bits such as 11111111 mean 255 unsigned but −1 in 8-bit two’s complement. For fixed-width registers, wrap-around and overflow flags use the Binary Calculator and Logic Gates page.

    Can I convert to decimal, hexadecimal and octal?

    Every operand and result is shown in binary, decimal, hexadecimal and octal, with the place-value expansion for short numbers (1010 = 8 + 2 = 10). To enter decimal, hexadecimal or octal values, or to convert fractions, use the Number System Converter.

    Is anything sent to a server?

    No. The calculation runs in your browser, and the history list is stored only in your browser.
