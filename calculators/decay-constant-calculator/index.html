    The formulas

    One decay law, Nₜ = N₀ × 2^(−t / T½) = N₀ × e^(−λt), written three ways:

    To findFormulaValid when
    Decay constant from half-lifeλ = ln 2 / T½T½ > 0
    Half-life from decay constantT½ = ln 2 / λλ > 0
    Mean lifetime from decay constantτ = 1 / λλ > 0
    Decay constant from mean lifetimeλ = 1 / ττ > 0
    Mean lifetime from half-lifeτ = T½ / ln 2 = T½ × 1.442695…T½ > 0
    Half-life from mean lifetimeT½ = τ × ln 2 = τ × 0.693147…τ > 0

    Each conversion is a single multiplication or division, so the result is exact to double precision. The mean lifetime is taken from T½ / ln 2 directly, not by inverting a rounded decay constant.

    What the three numbers mean

    QuantityMeaningUsed for
    Half-life T½Time for half of the quantity to decayReading a decay curve, comparing processes, quoting a nuclide
    Decay constant λFraction of the remaining quantity that decays per unit time (continuous form)Writing Nₜ = N₀ e^(−λt), rate equations, activity A = λN
    Mean lifetime τAverage life of one nucleus or particle; time to fall to 1/eTime constants, lifetimes of excited states, the electrical analogy (RC, L/R)

    Setting Nₜ = N₀ / 2 in Nₜ = N₀ e^(−λt) gives e^(−λT½) = 1/2, so T½ = ln 2 / λ. The mean lifetime is the average of t over the decaying nuclei, τ = ∫ t λ e^(−λt) dt = 1 / λ. Hence τ = T½ / ln 2.

    How much is left after a mean lifetime

    After one mean lifetime the quantity has fallen to 1/e, not to one half, because τ is longer than T½:

    Rules and limits

    • The half-life, decay constant and mean lifetime must each be greater than zero.
    • All three share one time unit; the decay constant is per that unit. “Also show in” changes the unit of all three together.
    • If you type more than one value, the half-life is used first, then the decay constant; the others are compared with it and a note is shown when they do not agree to one part in a billion.
    • Results are double-precision. A result that would be too large to represent is refused with a note to change the time unit, and one below about 2.2 × 10⁻³⁰⁸ is shown with a note that it carries fewer digits.
    • Mathematical decay only: not a radiation-safety, dosimetry or exposure assessment.

    Questions

    What is the relationship between half-life and decay constant?

    λ = ln 2 / T½. For a 10-year half-life λ = 0.0693147 per year. A longer half-life means a smaller decay constant, and the two are always inversely proportional.

    What is the mean lifetime, and why is it longer than the half-life?

    The half-life is the time for half to decay. The mean lifetime τ = 1 / λ is the average time a single nucleus survives, and also the time for the quantity to fall to 1/e = 36.7879%. It is always longer than the half-life, by the factor 1 / ln 2 = 1.4427. For a 10-year half-life τ = 14.427 years. Why is the mean lifetime longer than the half-life? Half of the nuclei are gone after one half-life, but the ones that last longest last much longer, and they raise the average. The decay law is skewed: many short lives and a few very long ones.

    Which should I use, the half-life or the decay constant?

    Quote the half-life when you want a feel for the time scale, and use the decay constant in formulas written with e^(−λt) or in rate equations. The calculator gives all three, so you can move between them.

    What do I enter if I know two of the three?

    One is enough. If you type more than one, the calculator uses the half-life first, then the decay constant, and tells you if the others do not agree with it. Choose “Start from” to use a particular one and ignore the rest.

    Why does the calculator say my values do not agree?

    Because they differ by more than one part in a billion. A decay constant of 0.0693147 and a half-life of 10 years differ in the seventh digit (ln 2 / 10 = 0.06931471806), so a rounded value is reported, not hidden. The half-life you typed is the one used.

    What unit is the decay constant in, and how do I change the time unit?

    Per unit of time, in the unit you choose: per second, per year and so on. If T½ is in years, λ is per year. The time unit applies to all three values together, so they cannot be mixed by mistake. Years are Julian years of 365.25 days. How do I change the time unit? Use “Also show in”. Half-life and mean lifetime are multiplied by the ratio of the units and the decay constant is divided by it. For example 6 hours is 21,600 seconds and λ = 3.20901 × 10⁻⁵ per second.

    Can I use this for RC and RL circuits?

    The mathematics is the same: the time constant RC or L/R plays the part of the mean lifetime, and the half-value time is RC ln 2 or (L/R) ln 2. Enter the time constant as the mean lifetime to get the half-value time and the decay rate. The physical processes are different, and the RC and RL Transient calculator keeps them labelled.

    Does the uncertainty of a measured half-life carry through?

    No. The calculator treats every number as exact. Convert the two ends of a measured range to see the spread.

    Is this a radiation safety or dose calculator?

    No. It converts three descriptions of the same exponential law. It does not give a dose, an exposure or a safety assessment.
