Unbalance Formulas: Voltage and Neutral Current

Three-phase unbalance formulas: percent voltage unbalance, neutral current from unequal phase currents and a table of neutral current, with worked examples.

The formulas

To findFormula
Average voltageVavg = (V1 + V2 + V3) / 3
Voltage unbalance (percent)max |V − Vavg| / Vavg × 100
Neutral current, resistive loadsIN = √(Ia² + Ib² + Ic² − Ia×Ib − Ib×Ic − Ic×Ia)

The voltage formula is the NEMA definition, using the line-to-line voltages. The IEC definition uses the negative-sequence component, which needs the phase angles as well. The neutral current formula assumes three currents 120° apart with the same power factor in each phase.

Worked example: voltage unbalance

The line voltages are 400 V, 410 V and 396 V.

  1. Vavg = (400 + 410 + 396) / 3 = 402 V
  2. The largest deviation is 8 V (the 410 V line)
  3. Unbalance = 8 / 402 × 100 = 1.99%

Worked example: neutral current

The phase currents are 100 A, 80 A and 60 A.

  1. IN = √(100² + 80² + 60² − 100×80 − 80×60 − 60×100) = √1,200 = 34.64 A

Frequently asked questions

Why does unbalance matter for motors?

A small voltage unbalance causes a much larger current unbalance in an induction motor. The extra current heats the windings, so the motor is derated as unbalance rises.

What causes voltage unbalance?

Unequal single-phase loads, a blown fuse on a capacitor bank, uneven transformer taps and loose connections.

Why is the neutral current zero for equal loads?

The three currents are equal in size and 120° apart, so their sum is zero.

Does the neutral formula include harmonics?

No. Third harmonic currents add in the neutral on top of this. See the harmonics page.
