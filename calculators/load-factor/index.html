Limits of this calculator

A low load factor means the supply and the demand charge are sized for a peak that is rare; moving or spreading load, or peak-shaving, raises it. A load factor over about 0.9 means a flat load that leaves little room to add load without raising the peak. Use the same period for the energy and the maximum demand: a month of energy with a year-high peak gives a load factor that is too low.

What this calculator checks

  • Maximum demand (connected load times demand factor, or a measured value), average demand from energy and hours, load factor, demand factor, the peak-above-average gap, the energy at the maximum demand and the unused share, against independent vectors built from metered demand profiles (energy by summing intervals, maximum as the largest interval).
  • The diversity factor from the sum of the feeders' own maxima over the maximum of their summed profile, including the guide's worked example (350 kW, 233.3 kW average, load factor 0.6667, diversity 1.2).
  • The refusal of an average demand above the maximum demand (load factor above 1), and the warnings for a low or very flat load factor, a diversity factor below 1, a sum of maxima above the connected load and a maximum demand above the connected load.

What remains unverified

  • That the energy and the maximum demand come from the same period and point of supply, and the demand interval of the utility (15 or 30 minutes) behind the maximum demand.
  • Demand factors from a code or utility table: the demand factor is user data and no table value is applied.
  • Tariff effects such as demand charges, ratchets and power-factor penalties, and the cost of a low load factor.
  • Load shape, coincidence between feeders and the load duration curve: only the three summary figures (energy, maximum and sum of maxima) are used.

Load Factor Formula

Maximum demand Pmd = connected load × demand factor (or measured) Average demand Pavg = energy / hours\nLoad factor = Pavg / Pmd = energy / (Pmd × hours) Demand factor = Pmd / connected load\nDiversity factor = sum of individual maximum demands / Pmd

Load Factor Worked Example

InputValue
Connected load500 kW
Demand factor0.7
Energy used in the period168,000 kWh
Length of the period720 h
Sum of the individual maximum demands (optional)420 kW
  1. Maximum demand = 500 × 0.7 = 350 kW
  2. Average demand = 168,000 kWh / 720 h = 233.33 kW
  3. Load factor = 233.33 / 350 = 0.6667 (66.67 %)
  4. Diversity factor = 420 / 350 = 1.2

Assumptions and Limitations

  • The energy and the maximum demand cover the same period and the same point of supply
  • The maximum demand is the highest average over the utility demand interval (often 15 or 30 minutes), not an instantaneous peak
  • The load factor is at most 1 and the diversity factor is at least 1; a result outside these is rejected or flagged
  • The demand factor is user data; the calculator does not supply code values

A low load factor means the supply and the demand charge are sized for a peak that is rare; moving or spreading load, or peak-shaving, raises it. A load factor over about 0.9 means a flat load that leaves little room to add load without raising the peak. Use the same period for the energy and the maximum demand: a month of energy with a year-high peak gives a load factor that is too low.

How to Calculate Load Factor

Each input is converted to the base unit of its quantity, the formula above is applied in the order shown in the worked example, and each result is shown in the unit you choose. Results are held at full precision and rounded only for display, except where a step selects a standard size, as the worked example shows. A value outside its allowed range is refused with a message instead of giving a result.

The load factor is at most 1 and the diversity factor is at least 1; a result outside these is rejected or flagged. The demand factor is user data; the calculator does not supply code values.
