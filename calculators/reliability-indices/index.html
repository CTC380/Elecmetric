Limits of this calculator

Use this to turn an outage log into the indices a regulator or utility reports. SAIFI says how often the average customer lost supply, SAIDI how long they were without it in total, and CAIDI how long a typical interruption lasted; CAIDI is what restoration crews influence, while SAIFI reflects how many faults there were and how many customers each reaches. A few large events usually dominate SAIDI, which is why the largest single event's share is shown. Reporting rules differ between regulators on what counts as an interruption, how momentary events and major event days are treated, and how customers are counted, so check the definitions your reporting follows before comparing figures. This is a calculation from the events you list; it does not model the network or predict future reliability.

What this calculator checks

  • SAIFI, SAIDI, CAIDI, ASAI and ASUI from up to five interruption events (customers interrupted and duration each), the customers served and the reporting period, using the customer-weighted definitions.
  • Customer interruptions, customer-minutes lost, the energy not supplied (and per customer) from one average load per interrupted customer, and the largest single event's share of SAIDI.
  • That no event interrupts more customers than are served or lasts longer than the period, that customers interrupted have a duration, that the customer-time lost does not exceed what all customers could lose, and a warning for any event under 5 minutes and for SAIFI or SAIDI above a target you enter.

What remains unverified

  • IEEE Std 1366 or any regulator's rules: momentary interruptions (under 5 minutes) and major event days are not excluded, MAIFI and the load-based indices (ASIFI, ASIDI) are not calculated, and no regulatory target or incentive scheme is applied.
  • The customer counts and durations: they are your records. Partial restoration in stages, planned versus unplanned outages and the cause of each outage are not separated, and the average load for the energy not supplied is one constant figure.
  • Any prediction of future reliability: no failure rates, network model or restoration times are modelled; the indices describe the events entered.

Reliability Indices Formula

Ci = customers interrupted in event i ri = duration of event i NT = customers served T = reporting period\nSAIFI = Σ Ci / NT SAIDI = Σ (Ci × ri) / NT CAIDI = SAIDI / SAIFI = Σ (Ci × ri) / Σ Ci\nASAI = (NT × T − Σ Ci × ri) / (NT × T) ASUI = 1 − ASAI ENS = Σ Ci × ri × L AENS = ENS / NT (L = average load per interrupted customer)

Assumptions and Limitations

  • The indices are customer-weighted: every customer counts equally and SAIFI and SAIDI are averages over all customers served, including those never interrupted
  • Each event is one sustained interruption of a given number of customers for a given time; a customer interrupted twice is counted twice in SAIFI, which is the intended meaning
  • Every event entered is counted. IEEE Std 1366 leaves interruptions shorter than 5 minutes out of SAIFI and SAIDI (they are momentary) and excludes major event days; this calculator does neither, so enter only the events your reporting rules count
  • ASAI and ASUI use the reporting period you enter and treat every customer as supplied for the whole period apart from the events listed
  • The energy not supplied uses one average load for every interrupted customer, taken as constant over the outage; it is an estimate, not a metered value
  • The definitions are those of the usual distribution reliability indices; the targets are your own figures and no regulator's limits or standard's clauses are applied

How to Calculate Reliability Indices

Each input is converted to the base unit of its quantity, the formula above is applied in the order shown in the worked example, and each result is shown in the unit you choose. Results are held at full precision and rounded only for display, except where a step selects a standard size, as the worked example shows. A value outside its allowed range is refused with a message instead of giving a result.
