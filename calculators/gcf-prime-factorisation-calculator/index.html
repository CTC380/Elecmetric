How to find the GCF by prime factorisation

Every whole number above 1 is a product of primes in exactly one way. Two numbers can share a factor only through the primes they have in common, so the GCF is built from those primes. The method has four steps.

  1. Factor each number into primes.
  2. Keep the primes that appear in every number. A prime missing from even one number cannot divide all of them.
  3. Take the lowest power of each kept prime. The GCF can use a prime only as many times as the number with the fewest copies of it.
  4. Multiply the results.

Both numbers contain 2 and 3. The lowest power of 2 is 22 (from 36) and the lowest power of 3 is 31 (from 24). So GCF = 22 × 3 = 4 × 3 = 12.

Finding the prime factors: the division ladder

To factor a number, divide by the smallest prime that goes in, then repeat on the quotient until it reaches 1. The divisors you used are the prime factors. For 24:

The divisors are 2, 2, 2 and 3, so 24 = 23 × 3. Choose “Also division ladders” in the calculator to see this for every number.

The same table gives the LCM

Lowest powers give the GCF. Highest powers give the least common multiple (LCM). For 24 and 36:

GCF = 22 × 3 = 12 and LCM = 23 × 32 = 72. For two numbers, GCF × LCM = 12 × 72 = 864 = 24 × 36.

A set such as 17 and 19 has no prime in common, so the GCF is 1 and the numbers are relatively prime (coprime). The numbers do not need to be prime themselves: 8 = 23 and 15 = 3 × 5 are coprime too. A set of three, 360, 840 and 1260, has GCF 60 = 2^2 × 3 × 5.

Common mistakes

  • Using the highest power instead of the lowest. That gives the LCM, not the GCF.
  • Keeping a prime that is not in every number. In GCF(16, 88, 104) the 11 is only in 88, so it is dropped.
  • Forgetting that a power can differ. 24 has one 3 but 36 has two; the GCF takes one.
  • Forgetting the answer 1. If no prime is shared, the GCF is 1, not “none”.

When to use prime factorisation, and when not to

Prime factorisation shows why a GCF is what it is, and it is easy to do by hand for small numbers. For very large numbers it is the wrong tool, because factoring a big number can take far longer than finding the GCF itself. The Euclidean algorithm finds the GCF without any factoring, and Mode 01 of this calculator uses it. This page does both: it factors every number in full up to 1018 and always checks the result against the Euclidean algorithm. Above that, the GCF is found by the Euclidean algorithm first and then only its own primes are shown, so the result stays exact.

Rules this calculator follows

  • The GCF is never negative. Signs are ignored, so GCF(−24, 36) = 12.
  • Zero is allowed. Every prime divides 0, so 0 has no prime factorisation and does not limit the GCF: GCF(0, 15) = 15 = 3 × 5. All zeros is undefined, because every integer divides 0.
  • 1 has no prime factors. GCF(1, 7) = 1.
  • Exact integers. Numbers are held in BigInt arithmetic up to 500 digits, with no floating-point rounding.
  • Primes are tested, not guessed. Large factors are found with Pollard’s rho method and each prime is confirmed with a Miller–Rabin test that is exact for numbers up to 1018.
  • Checked every time. The page confirms that each factorisation multiplies back to its number, that every factor is prime, that the lowest-power product equals the Euclidean result, that the GCF divides every number and that the leftover parts share no factor.

Questions

How do I find the GCF using prime factorisation?

Write each number as a product of primes, keep the primes that appear in every number, take the lowest power of each, and multiply. For 24 = 2^3 × 3 and 36 = 2^2 × 3^2 the GCF is 2^2 × 3 = 12.

Why the lowest power of each prime?

A common factor can use a prime only as many times as every number has it. The number with the fewest copies sets the limit, so the lowest power is the largest that fits all of them.

What if the numbers share no prime?

Then the only common factor is 1, so GCF = 1 and the numbers are relatively prime (coprime). For example 17 and 19, or 8 and 15.

How do I find the GCF of three or more numbers this way?

Factor every number, keep only the primes that appear in all of them, and take the lowest power of each across all the numbers. For 16, 88 and 104 the only shared prime is 2, with lowest power 3, so the GCF is 8.

What is the difference between the GCF and the LCM in this table?

The GCF uses the lowest power of each prime shared by all the numbers. The LCM uses the highest power of every prime that appears in any of the numbers.

Is GCF the same as GCD and HCF?

Yes. They are three names for the same value. This page serves all three names.

How large a number can it factor?

Every number up to 10^18 is factored in full. If a number is larger, the Euclidean algorithm finds the GCF first and the page shows the prime powers of the GCF only, so the answer is still exact.

What is the prime factorisation of 0 and 1?

0 has none, because every prime divides 0, so it is set aside. 1 is the empty product and has no prime factors. GCF(0, n) = |n| and GCF(1, n) = 1.
