How this was calculated

    BMI Formula

    BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

    The US customary form, BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)², is the same formula with a rounded conversion factor (the exact factor is about 703.07). This calculator converts every input to kg and metres first and uses the exact metric formula, so results do not depend on the unit you enter.

    Healthy weight range for a height H (in metres) is BMIlower × H² to BMIupper × H², using the limits of the selected reference. The upper limit is exclusive: a BMI exactly at the upper limit falls in the next category.

    Why the two references give different categories

    The international adult reference puts overweight at a BMI of 25 and above. The Indian reference (Endocrine Society of India, 2022 clinical practice guidelines, as supplied for this module) uses lower cut-offs, with overweight from 23 and obesity from 25. At a BMI of 24.0, for example, the international reference gives Normal and the Indian reference gives Overweight. This is a difference between reference systems, not a calculation error. Neither is a diagnosis.

    Limitations

    • BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis. It cannot tell muscle from fat and does not show where fat is carried.
    • It may mislead for athletes, very muscular people, older adults, and during pregnancy.
    • Waist circumference and waist-to-hip ratio give extra information, particularly for South Asian adults whose abdominal fat can be high at a normal BMI.
    • For ages under 20, adult categories do not apply. Use an age- and sex-specific child and teen BMI reference.
    • The healthy weight range is a reference range, not a personal weight target. Speak to a doctor or dietitian before making health decisions.
