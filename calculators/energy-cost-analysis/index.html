What this calculator checks

  • The monthly energy of up to twelve appliance rows (number of units x power of one unit x share of rating drawn x hours per day x days), computed by the Multiple Appliance Electricity Cost calculator, and the largest user.
  • The slab bill for those units (telescopic or non-telescopic energy charge, fixed monthly charge, duty and tax as percentages of energy and fixed charge, other charges) computed by the Electricity Bill from Meter Readings calculator, and the average cost per kWh.
  • The bill again after one chosen row is replaced (the energy saved by the Equipment Replacement Energy Savings calculator), the bill saving as the difference of the two bills, the simple payback and the net saving over the life; rejection of slab limits out of order, of a replaced row with no power, of hours above 24 a day, of a negative power and of a fractional number of units.

What remains unverified

  • The appliance powers, hours and shares of rating, the slab limits, rates and charges, the cost of the new equipment and the service it gives; these are your data and decide the result.
  • Sanctioned-load or demand charges, per-unit surcharges, time-of-use rates, subsidies, rebates, minimum charges, power factor penalties and arrears; the planner prices one flat set of slabs. A printed bill is usually higher than the list because the meter also records appliances that are not listed.
  • Seasonal or monthly patterns (use Multiple Appliance Electricity Cost for the yearly energy), tariff change over the life, discounting, maintenance and a replacement that gives a different cooling, light or output.

Electricity Cost Planner Formula

Step 1: monthly energy E = Σ n × P × share × hours × days (Multiple Appliance Electricity Cost) Step 2: bill = slab energy charge + fixed charge, then duty and tax, plus other charges (Electricity Bill from Meter Readings, units = E); or, for a time-of-use tariff, E split into peak, shoulder and off-peak at the shares entered, plus billed demand × demand rate (Time-of-Use and Demand-Charge Bill) Step 3: replacement saves dE = n × (Pold × share × hours − Pnew × share × hours) × days; bill after = bill at (E − dE) units; saving = bill − bill after Payback = investment / bill saving per month Net = saving × 12 × life − investment

Assumptions and Limitations

  • Each appliance row is n identical units drawing the share of their rating entered, for the hours entered, every day of the billing month
  • The units billed are exactly the energy of the list. A real meter also records appliances that are not in the list, so a printed bill is usually higher: enter the meter units in Electricity Bill from Meter Readings to compare
  • The bill uses one set of slabs for the month, with a fixed monthly charge, duty and tax as percentages of energy and fixed charge, and other charges added at the end. No sanctioned-load charge, per-unit surcharge, subsidy or rebate. A time-of-use tariff splits the list energy by one set of peak and shoulder shares, or by a share for each appliance if you choose that, so a share is a fixed fraction of the energy of an appliance, not a schedule of hours; the recorded demand is an input from the meter, not worked out from the list. After a replacement the energy saved is taken off in the shares of the replaced appliance (or of the whole list)
  • The saving is the difference between two bills, so it uses the slab the replaced energy was billed at. It is therefore not simply the saved kWh times the average rate
  • Annual figures are twelve bills like this one, not a seasonal profile; for seasonal or monthly hours use Multiple Appliance Electricity Cost for the yearly energy
  • Payback and net saving are simple and not discounted; they ignore tariff change, maintenance and any difference in the service the equipment gives

Slab tariffs make the saving depend on where the household sits in the slabs: removing units from the highest slab saves at the highest rate. Check the slab limits and rates against your tariff order or your printed bill, and whether your utility bills telescopically. A replacement is only a like-for-like saving if it gives the same cooling, light or output. For a printed bill that is far above the list, an appliance is missing, the hours are low, or the tariff has charges this planner does not model.

How to Calculate Electricity Cost Planner

Each input is converted to the base unit of its quantity, the formula above is applied in the order shown in the worked example, and each result is shown in the unit you choose. Results are held at full precision and rounded only for display, except where a step selects a standard size, as the worked example shows. A value outside its allowed range is refused with a message instead of giving a result.

What does this calculator assume?

Each appliance row is n identical units drawing the share of their rating entered, for the hours entered, every day of the billing month. The units billed are exactly the energy of the list. A real meter also records appliances that are not in the list, so a printed bill is usually higher: enter the meter units in Electricity Bill from Meter Readings to compare. The bill uses one set of slabs for the month, with a fixed monthly charge, duty and tax as percentages of energy and fixed charge, and other charges added at the end. No sanctioned-load charge, per-unit surcharge, subsidy or rebate. A time-of-use tariff splits the list energy by one set of peak and shoulder shares, or by a share for each appliance if you choose that, so a share is a fixed fraction of the energy of an appliance, not a schedule of hours; the recorded demand is an input from the meter, not worked out from the list. After a replacement the energy saved is taken off in the shares of the replaced appliance (or of the whole list). The saving is the difference between two bills, so it uses the slab the replaced energy was billed at. It is therefore not simply the saved kWh times the average rate. Annual figures are twelve bills like this one, not a seasonal profile; for seasonal or monthly hours use Multiple Appliance Electricity Cost for the yearly energy. Payback and net saving are simple and not discounted; they ignore tariff change, maintenance and any difference in the service the equipment gives.
