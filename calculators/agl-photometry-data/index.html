Limits of this calculator

Use this to turn a photometric measurement of an airfield light into a pass or fail against your own figures: enter the readings, the average you require, the lowest share of the average any point may have, the average of a new light and the lowest light output you accept in service. The uncertainty line shows when a light that passes on the readings would not pass in the worst case, and the light output line shows how far an in-service light has fallen from new. The calculator does not decide where the points go or what the numbers should be: the point pattern, the required intensities and the in-service limits come from the specification for the light type, such as the ICAO Annex 14 light characteristics, the aerodrome design manual for visual aids and the manufacturer data, and must be checked in the current edition. For the intensity seen at the pilot's eye through fog use the Allard's law calculator; for the lux a luminaire gives on a surface use the point-by-point calculator.

About this calculator

This page calculates average intensity, lowest intensity, highest intensity and 15 more results from what the readings are, measuring distance, number of readings used, reading 1 and 16 more inputs. Change any value or its unit and the result updates. The steps below show the working for the default values, so you can check each line against your own method.

What this calculator checks

  • The conversion of lux readings at a stated distance to candela with I = E x d^2, and the average, lowest and highest of up to 12 readings, the first lowest and first highest point, Imin / Iavg, Imax / Iavg, the standard deviation (divisor n) and the coefficient of variation.
  • The number of points below the lowest allowed point (the user's share of the average), whether the average meets the user's required average and by how much it falls short, and the average with the user's measuring uncertainty taken off.
  • The average as a percentage of the average of a new light, and whether it meets the user's in-service limit.

What remains unverified

  • Where the measuring points are, how many there are, the grid or isocandela pattern, the size of the main beam, the setting of the light and the aiming of the photometer: the points and their pattern come from the user's method, and no solid-angle weighting is applied, so the average is a plain mean of the readings entered.
  • The required average, the lowest allowed point, the average of a new light and the in-service limit: they are the user's figures, are not looked up, and no ICAO, FAA or other photometric requirement is applied. The photometric requirements for the light type, and their current edition, must be checked in the source.
  • Colour, the inverse-square conversion's own conditions (a distance of at least five times the largest size of the light and a photometer facing the light squarely are not checked), photometer calibration, stray light, supply current and lamp condition during the test, and any full measurement uncertainty budget: the uncertainty is one worst-case percentage taken off the average.

AGL Photometry Data Formula

Lux to candela: I = E × d² (E in lux at distance d in metres)\nIavg = (I1 + I2 + … + In) / n Imin, Imax, standard deviation σ = √( Σ (Ii − Iavg)² / n ) coefficient of variation = σ / Iavg × 100\nLowest allowed point = Fp / 100 × Iavg points below it are counted\nShortfall = max(0, Ireq − Iavg) average with uncertainty = Iavg × (1 − u / 100)\nLight output remaining = Iavg / Inew × 100

Assumptions and Limitations

  • The readings are taken at the points your method prescribes within the main beam, each point counted once; the average is the simple mean of those points, not weighted by the solid angle each stands for
  • Lux readings are converted with the inverse-square law, I = E × d², which holds only when the distance is at least five times the largest size of the light and the photometer faces the light squarely; the calculator cannot check either
  • The standard deviation is over the n points entered (divisor n), not an estimate for a larger population
  • The required average, the point limit, the average of a new light and the in-service limit are your own figures; none is looked up or taken from a standard, and each requirement is a plain comparison with them
  • The uncertainty is applied as a single worst-case percentage taken off the average; it is not an uncertainty budget and it does not change the lowest or highest point

How to Calculate AGL Photometry Data

Each input is converted to the base unit of its quantity, the formula above is applied in the order shown in the worked example, and each result is shown in the unit you choose. Results are held at full precision and rounded only for display, except where a step selects a standard size, as the worked example shows. A value outside its allowed range is refused with a message instead of giving a result.
